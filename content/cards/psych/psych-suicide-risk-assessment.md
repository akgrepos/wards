## At a Glance
- A positive screen starts a structured assessment of thoughts, plan, intent, past behavior, symptoms, support and reasons for living. The screen alone does not set the disposition. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa, horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
- Thoughts of suicide right now mean imminent risk. The patient needs an urgent psychiatric evaluation and is not left alone. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- On the {{calc:c-ssrs}}, ideation with intent in the past month (with or without a plan), or suicidal behavior in the past 3 months, is high risk and starts the admission process. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- No tool replaces clinical judgment. Risk assessments and prediction models are poor at predicting who will die by suicide. ^[acep-adult-psychiatric-2017, vadod-suicide-2024, columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- High acute risk typically needs psychiatric hospitalization, direct observation and an environment with limited access to lethal means. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Reducing access to lethal means carries a weak recommendation. Safety planning is standard care at intermediate or high risk, though its evidence is insufficient for a formal recommendation. ^[vadod-suicide-2024, stanley-spi-2018]

## First Steps
- Ask about thoughts of killing yourself right now. A yes needs an urgent mental health evaluation, and the patient cannot be left alone. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- Page psychiatry urgently and keep the patient safe in the emergency department or on the unit. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- With intent (with or without a plan) or suicidal behavior in the past 3 months, stay with the patient until transfer to a higher level of care is complete. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Interview the patient alone. Ask visitors to leave the room. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- Keep the room free of sharps, cords, tubing and toxic substances. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

## Severity
- Triage with the {{calc:c-ssrs}} (SAFE-T) places the patient from the answers: ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]

:::scale Risk level, SAFE-T ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Low: Wish to die or ideation without method, intent, plan or behavior (items 1 or 2). Or modifiable risk factors and strong protective factors. Or no history of ideation or behavior. Action: Outpatient referral ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Moderate: Ideation with a method but no plan, intent or behavior in the past month (item 3). Or suicidal behavior more than 3 months ago. Or multiple risk factors and few protective factors. Action: Use clinical judgment to decide if further evaluation is needed. Outpatient referral ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- High: Ideation with intent, or intent with a plan, in the past month (items 4 or 5). Or suicidal behavior within the past 3 months. Action: Start the psychiatric admission process. Stay with the patient until transfer. Document the outcome of the emergency psychiatric evaluation ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
:::

- The VA/DoD guideline gives an example scheme that separates acute risk from chronic risk. It found no evidence to favor one stratification tool over another. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

:::scale Acute risk, VA/DoD ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Low: Possible ideation, no current intent, no specific current plan, no recent preparatory behavior. Patient, clinician and family are confident the patient can stay safe. Action: Reduce chronic risk. Outpatient treatment if indicated ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Intermediate: Ideation, and able to keep self safe independently. Often no intent because of a reason for living, and able to follow a safety plan. Action: Consider voluntary hospitalization if the drivers of risk respond to inpatient treatment. Otherwise outpatient care with frequent contact, reassessment, a safety plan and lethal means counseling ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- High: Ideation with intent, and unable to keep self safe without outside help. Often a plan, access to lethal means or a recent attempt. Action: Typically psychiatric hospitalization, voluntary or involuntary ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
:::

- Chronic risk: ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - High: chronic medical or mental health conditions, chronic pain, chronic ideation, prior attempts, substance use disorder, few reasons for living, few coping skills, unstable psychosocial status. Needs routine follow-up, a well-developed safety plan and lethal means counseling. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - Intermediate: the same conditions, with protective factors and relative stability that suggest the patient can endure a future crisis. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - Low: little or no mental health or substance use problem, or illness with strong resources. Usually no history of self-directed violence, chronic ideation or marked impulsivity. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

## Causes
- **Suicidal thoughts and behavior:** current ideation, intent, a plan, past suicide attempts and self-injury. Past behavior is the strongest predictor of future attempts. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa, vadod-suicide-2024, uspstf-depression-suicide-2023]
- **Psychiatric illness:** any mental disorder (odds ratio 13.1 for suicide), depression (11.0), schizophrenia spectrum disorder (7.8), bipolar disorder (4.6) and personality disorders (3.4 for antisocial to 9.0 for borderline). ^[vadod-suicide-2024, favril-suicide-risk-factors-2022]
- **Past self-harm:** odds ratio 10.1 for suicide. ^[favril-suicide-risk-factors-2022]
- **Clinical status:** hopelessness, major depressive or mixed episode, command hallucinations to hurt self, chronic pain or another acute medical problem, impulsivity, substance use, agitation or severe anxiety, perceived burden on others, aggression toward others, refusing a safety plan. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- **Activating events:** recent losses or other major negative events, pending incarceration or homelessness, isolation. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- **Treatment history:** earlier psychiatric diagnosis and treatment, hopelessness about treatment, nonadherence, no current treatment, insomnia. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- **Lethal means:** ask specifically about a firearm in the home or ease of getting one. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- **Background:** family history of suicide, lifetime sexual abuse, family violence or abuse, incarceration or legal problems, being a veteran and being male raise risk. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026, uspstf-depression-suicide-2023]

## Presentation
- Direct warning signs: ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - Suicide-related communication, such as a note or a stated wish to die ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - Preparation, such as giving items away ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
  - Seeking access to or recent use of lethal means ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Indirect warning signs: increasing substance use, hopelessness, no sense of purpose, anger, recklessness, feeling trapped, social withdrawal, agitation, dramatic mood change, sleep disturbance, guilt or shame. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Protective factors: reasons for living, fear of death or of pain, responsibility to family, a supportive social network, work or school, access to mental health care, problem-solving skills, a sense of purpose. They may not counteract significant acute risk factors. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026, vadod-suicide-2024]
- Among 727 medical inpatients, 4.8% screened positive on the reference questionnaire and only 1 patient endorsed current suicidal thoughts. Most positive screens are non-acute. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]

## Diagnosis
- Screening is a validated brief tool. The ASQ has four questions, and the {{calc:c-ssrs}} screener is the other option. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020, vadod-suicide-2024]
- ASQ questions: ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
  - In the past few weeks, have you wished you were dead? ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
  - In the past few weeks, have you felt that you or your family would be better off if you were dead? ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
  - In the past week, have you been having thoughts about killing yourself? ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
  - Have you ever tried to kill yourself? ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
- A yes to any item is a positive screen. A yes to "Are you having thoughts of killing yourself right now?" is an acute positive, which means imminent risk. All positive screens get a brief suicide safety assessment. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020, nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- In 727 adult medical and surgical inpatients at four hospitals, the ASQ had sensitivity 100% (95% CI 90% to 100%), specificity 89% (86% to 91%), negative predictive value 100% and positive predictive value 32%. It is one validation study with few positives. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
- {{calc:c-ssrs}} questions, each for the past month: ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
  - 1. Wished you were dead or could go to sleep and not wake up ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
  - 2. Actual thoughts of killing yourself ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
  - 3. Thinking about how you might do it, with no specific plan or intent ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
  - 4. Thoughts with some intention of acting on them ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
  - 5. Worked out the details of a plan, with intent to carry it out ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- If the answer to question 2 is no, skip questions 3, 4 and 5. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Ask about suicidal behavior: "Have you ever done anything, started to do anything, or prepared to do anything to end your life?" Examples are collecting pills, obtaining a gun, giving away valuables and actual attempts. If yes, ask whether it was within the past 3 months. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- For the most severe ideation, grade frequency, duration, controllability, deterrents and the reason for the ideation. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Brief suicide safety assessment after a positive screen, done by a clinician: ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
  - Frequency of thoughts, and whether they are present right now ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
  - Plan, method and access to means. A detailed or feasible plan is a reason for greater concern and for removing or securing dangerous items. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
  - Past self-injury and attempts: method, date, intent and treatment received ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
  - Symptoms: depression, anxiety, impulsivity, hopelessness, isolation, irritability, substance use ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
  - Support and safety: a trusted person, reasons for not killing yourself, and "Do you think you need help to keep yourself safe?" A no does not mean the patient is safe, and a yes needs action now. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- Document the risk level, the warning signs, risk indicators and protective factors, access to lethal means, collateral sources, the data behind the risk level, and the reason for the actions taken or not taken. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- The Joint Commission requires a validated screening tool for patients 12 and older treated mainly for a behavioral health condition. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
- Any patient who screens positive or voices suicidal thoughts during care gets an evidence-based assessment. It asks directly about ideation, plan, intent, suicidal or self-harm behavior, risk factors and protective factors. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
- Document the overall risk level and the plan to lower the risk. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
- In emergency department patients with suicidal ideation, do not use risk-assessment tools alone to find low-risk patients who are safe for discharge. A psychiatric assessment and clinical judgment that weigh patient, family and community factors decide risk. ^[acep-adult-psychiatric-2017]
- Risk assessments and prediction models are poor at predicting who will die by suicide, but they show what contributes to the risk and guide the plan. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Screening programs: the VA/DoD finds the evidence insufficient to recommend for or against them. The USPSTF gives screening adults for suicide risk an I statement, for adults without a mental health diagnosis or signs of risk. Repeated screening does not appear to increase suicidal behavior, with low confidence. ^[vadod-suicide-2024, uspstf-depression-suicide-2023]

## Treatment
1. Keep the patient safe
   - Imminent risk: urgent psychiatric evaluation, and keep the patient safe in the emergency department or on the unit. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
   - High risk on the {{calc:c-ssrs}}: start the local psychiatric admission process and stay with the patient until transfer. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
   - High acute risk: direct observation on a secure unit, with sharps, cords, tubing and toxic substances out of reach. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Involuntary hospitalization follows local procedures when psychiatric hospitalization is indicated to maintain safety. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

2. Choose the observation level
   - The observation level follows the hospital's written policy for monitoring high-risk patients. The Joint Commission requires the policy but does not set the level. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
   - On medical units, high-risk measures include one-to-one monitoring, removing objects that could be used for self-harm when care allows, checking items visitors bring and safe transport within the hospital. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
   - Medical units need not be ligature resistant, but remove objects that could be used for self-harm from around a high-risk patient when possible. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
   - In a national inquiry of 113 suicides by mental health inpatients under observation, 5 occurred under constant observation and most under intermittent observation. Deaths were linked to less experienced staff, staff unfamiliar with the patient, deviation from procedures and absconding. ^[flynn-inpatient-observation-2017]
   - The authors advise experienced staff and observation levels set by clinical need, not by staffing. ^[flynn-inpatient-observation-2017]

3. Reduce access to lethal means
   - Lethal means safety is a collaborative, voluntary practice that limits access to objects that could be used for self-harm. More time and distance between a person with suicidal intent and the means lowers risk. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Reducing access to lethal means is a weak recommendation. Observational evidence links it to fewer suicides by firearm, jumping and medication overdose. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Securing medications, guns and ropes is part of the plan when the patient has a feasible method. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]

4. Safety plan before discharge
   - The Safety Planning Intervention has 6 steps: personal warning signs, internal coping strategies, people and places that distract, people who can help in a crisis, mental health professionals and urgent services to contact, and lethal means counseling. ^[stanley-spi-2018]
   - In a non-randomized cohort of 1,640 emergency department patients, the safety plan with follow-up calls was linked to fewer suicidal behaviors over 6 months (3.03% vs 5.29%, odds ratio 0.56). It was also linked to more outpatient mental health visits. ^[stanley-spi-2018]
   - The VA/DoD finds the evidence insufficient to recommend for or against a crisis response plan or safety planning to reduce suicide attempts. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - It still treats the safety plan as standard care and encourages it at intermediate or high acute or chronic risk. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Hospitals need written procedures for counseling and follow-up care at discharge for patients at risk. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]
   - With no further evaluation needed in the emergency department, create a safety plan for future suicidal thoughts and discuss securing or removing dangerous items. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]

5. Follow-up contact
   - In the safety planning cohort, calls were attempted within 72 hours of discharge and then weekly. They covered a risk and mood check, review of the safety plan and help starting treatment. ^[stanley-spi-2018]
   - The VA/DoD finds the evidence insufficient for brief contact interventions after emergency department discharge. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Periodic caring communications, such as postal mail or text messages, for 12 months after hospitalization for suicide risk carry a weak recommendation. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Give every patient crisis resources: the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline (call or text 988) and the Crisis Text Line (text HOME to 741-741). ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]

6. Treat what drives the risk
   - Modifiable risk factors, such as insomnia, can often be reduced. Examples are treating depression, lethal means counseling and strengthening social support. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
   - Manage co-occurring psychiatric symptoms during hospitalization. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

## Monitor
- Reassess risk by clinical judgment at low acute risk and at each outpatient contact for intermediate acute risk. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Update the safety plan and repeat lethal means counseling at follow-up for intermediate acute risk. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
- Chronic risk needs routine suicide risk assessment and mental health follow-up. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]

## Escalation
- Psychiatry, urgently: thoughts of suicide right now, intent (with or without a plan), or suicidal behavior in the past 3 months. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa, columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Full mental health and safety evaluation in the emergency department when further evaluation of risk is needed. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- A patient who screens non-acute positive and leaves before assessment is handled like a discharge against medical advice. Only acute positives are considered at imminent risk. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]

## More Information
### Evidence: Key studies
- ASQ in adult inpatients (2020, 727 patients): sensitivity 100%, specificity 89%, positive predictive value 32%. Cross-sectional validation against a 25-item questionnaire. ^[horowitz-asq-inpatient-2020]
- Safety Planning Intervention with follow-up (2018, 1,640 patients): 3.03% vs 5.29% suicidal behavior at 6 months. Non-randomized, in Veterans Health Administration emergency departments, mostly men. ^[stanley-spi-2018]
- Suicide under observation (2017, England and Wales, 2006 to 2012): 113 suicides, an average of 16 per year, in mental health inpatients under observation. ^[flynn-inpatient-observation-2017]
- USPSTF (2023): screening adults for suicide risk is an I statement (insufficient evidence). ^[uspstf-depression-suicide-2023]
- ACEP clinical policy (2017): do not use risk tools alone to find low-risk patients safe for discharge. Level C. ^[acep-adult-psychiatric-2017]
- Joint Commission hospital goal 8 (2026, replaced the 2019 patient safety goal 15.01.01): screening, assessment, documented risk and plan, monitoring policy, discharge counseling and follow-up. ^[tjc-npg8-suicide-2026]

## Pearls
- A no to "do you need help to stay safe" does not mean the patient is safe. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- Past suicidal behavior is the strongest predictor of future attempts. ^[nimh-asq-adult-ed-bssa]
- Protective factors may not outweigh significant acute risk factors. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Ask about a firearm in the home by name. ^[columbia-safet-cssrs-2026]
- Firearms account for 50% of suicide deaths in the United States, and about 90% of attempts with a firearm end in death. ^[vadod-suicide-2024]
