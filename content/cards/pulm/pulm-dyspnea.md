## At a Glance
- First minutes: airway, vital signs with {{calc:news2|NEWS2}} if useful, SpO₂ and work of breathing, monitor and intravenous access. Give oxygen to target while looking for the cause. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, common-practice]
- Target SpO₂ 94–98%. 88–92% when at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- Cannot-miss causes: pulmonary edema, pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, acute coronary syndrome, upper airway obstruction or anaphylaxis, severe asthma or COPD, pneumonia with sepsis, tamponade, metabolic acidosis. ^[common-practice]
- Core tests: ECG, chest radiograph, blood gas, troponin, BNP or NT-proBNP. Add bedside ultrasound when the diagnosis is uncertain. ^[acp-pocus-2021, esc-hf-2026, common-practice]
- Suspected PE: probability tool, then {{calc:perc}} or D-dimer, then CT pulmonary angiography. See [[pulm-vte-diagnosis]]. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Failing oxygen or rising work of breathing: high-flow nasal oxygen or noninvasive ventilation, ICU review. See [[crit-hypoxemic-respiratory-failure]]. ^[ats-nirs-2026]

## Definition and Staging
- Dyspnea is a symptom with no single cause. Many patients have more than one (for example heart failure and COPD, or pneumonia and PE). ^[common-practice]

:::scale Triage by first-minutes findings
- Stable: normal mentation, speaking full sentences, SpO₂ at target on low-flow oxygen or room air. Proceeds to the targeted workup. ^[common-practice]
- Impending failure: tachypnea, accessory muscle use, speaking in short phrases, rising oxygen needs. ICU review and a blood gas now. ^[common-practice]
- Unstable: airway threat, exhaustion, altered mentation, shock, or hypoxemia despite oxygen. Resuscitate while diagnosing. ^[common-practice]
:::

## First Steps
1. Airway and breathing
   - Can the patient speak, handle secretions and keep the airway open? Stridor, drooling, lip or tongue swelling, or a muffled voice: call anesthesia or the airway team at once. ^[common-practice]
   - Oxygen to target: 94–98%, or 88–92% with risk of hypercapnia (COPD or other chronic lung disease, obesity hypoventilation, neuromuscular or chest wall disease). ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
2. Monitor
   - Continuous pulse oximetry and ECG monitoring, blood pressure, respiratory rate, temperature, glucose, intravenous access. ^[common-practice]
   - A normal SpO₂ does not exclude respiratory distress. A rising respiratory rate is the earlier sign of deterioration. ^[common-practice]
3. Look for immediately reversible causes
   - Absent breath sounds with hypotension: tension pneumothorax. Decompress without waiting for imaging. ^[common-practice]
   - Wheeze or prolonged expiration: bronchodilators. See [[pulm-copd-exacerbation]]. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - Stridor, urticaria or angioedema after an exposure: anaphylaxis or airway obstruction. ^[common-practice]
   - Pinpoint pupils with hypoventilation: opioid toxicity. ^[common-practice]
4. Draw tests while treating
   - Blood gas, ECG, troponin, BNP or NT-proBNP, CBC, BMP, and lactate when infection or shock is possible. ^[common-practice]
   - Portable chest radiograph. Bedside ultrasound if the diagnosis is uncertain or the patient is unstable. ^[acp-pocus-2021, common-practice]
5. Confirm code status and goals early. ^[common-practice]

## Causes
- **Cardiac:** [!cannot-miss] acute pulmonary edema from acute decompensated heart failure ([[cardio-adhf]]), acute coronary syndrome ([[cardio-chest-pain]]), arrhythmia such as rapid atrial fibrillation ([[cardio-atrial-fibrillation]]), acute valvular regurgitation, tamponade. ^[common-practice]
- **Vascular:** [!cannot-miss] pulmonary embolism ([[pulm-vte-diagnosis]]). Pulmonary hypertension crisis. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- **Airway:** [!cannot-miss] upper airway obstruction, angioedema, anaphylaxis, foreign body. [!most-common] asthma and COPD exacerbation ([[pulm-copd-exacerbation]]). ^[common-practice, gold-copd-2026]
- **Pulmonary parenchymal:** [!most-common] pneumonia ([[id-cap]]), aspiration, interstitial lung disease flare, acute respiratory distress syndrome ([[crit-hypoxemic-respiratory-failure]]). ^[common-practice]
- **Pleural:** [!cannot-miss] tension pneumothorax. Large pleural effusion or hemothorax. ^[common-practice]
- **Metabolic and hematologic:** metabolic acidosis (diabetic ketoacidosis, [[endo-dka]], uremia, [[renal-aki]], sepsis, salicylate toxicity), severe anemia, carbon monoxide poisoning, methemoglobinemia. ^[common-practice]
- **Neuromuscular:** Guillain-Barré syndrome, myasthenic crisis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, phrenic nerve injury, severe deconditioning. ^[common-practice]
- **Other:** anxiety or panic (a diagnosis of exclusion in the acute setting), pregnancy-related causes, obesity, deconditioning. ^[common-practice]

## Presentation
#### Focused history
- Onset (sudden, hours, days), trajectory, triggers, position (orthopnea, platypnea), exertional tolerance versus baseline. ^[common-practice]
- Associated chest pain, cough and sputum, fever, hemoptysis, leg swelling, palpitations, syncope, weight gain. ^[common-practice]
- Prior heart, lung or neuromuscular disease, home oxygen, recent medication change or missed doses (diuretics, inhalers). ^[common-practice]
- PE risk: immobility, surgery, cancer, prior VTE, estrogen, unilateral leg swelling. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Exposures: allergens, aspiration events, travel, sick contacts, smoking, opioids, sedatives. ^[common-practice]

#### Focused examination
- Appearance: speaking in sentences or words, tripod posture, accessory muscle use, paradoxical abdominal motion, diaphoresis, cyanosis. ^[common-practice]
- Neck: stridor, jugular venous distension, tracheal position. ^[common-practice]
- Chest: wheeze, crackles, unilateral decreased breath sounds, dullness, subcutaneous emphysema. ^[common-practice]
- Heart: gallop, new murmur, rub, muffled sounds, pulsus paradoxus. ^[common-practice]
- Legs and perfusion: unilateral edema, bilateral edema, cool extremities, capillary refill. ^[common-practice]
- Mentation: confusion or somnolence suggests hypercapnia or hypoxemia and is a warning sign. ^[common-practice]

## Diagnosis
#### Blood gas
- Arterial blood gas for oxygenation, PaCO₂ and pH when SpO₂ is low, mentation is altered, or hypercapnia is possible. ^[common-practice]
- Venous gas is reasonable for pH. A normal venous PCO₂ makes arterial hypercapnia unlikely, a raised one needs arterial confirmation. It gives no oxygenation data. ^[common-practice]
- Suspected carbon monoxide poisoning or methemoglobinemia: co-oximetry. Pulse oximetry is falsely reassuring. ^[common-practice]
- Metabolic acidosis with compensatory tachypnea: calculate the {{calc:anion-gap|anion gap}}. See [[endo-dka]]. ^[common-practice]

#### ECG, radiograph and laboratory
- 12-lead ECG: ischemia, arrhythmia, right heart strain, low voltage with effusion. ^[common-practice]
- Chest radiograph: pulmonary edema, consolidation, effusion, pneumothorax, hyperinflation. A normal radiograph points to PE, airway disease or noncardiopulmonary causes. ^[common-practice]
- Troponin: raised in ACS but also in heart failure, PE, sepsis and kidney disease. Interpret with the rise or fall and the picture. ^[udmi-2026]
- Hemoglobin, BMP, lactate and glucose for anemia, acidosis, kidney injury and sepsis. ^[common-practice]

#### Natriuretic peptides
- Measure BNP or NT-proBNP when heart failure is possible and the diagnosis is uncertain. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- Rule-out: BNP below 100 pg/mL or NT-proBNP below 300 pg/mL makes acute heart failure unlikely. Ask for echocardiography if clinical suspicion stays high. ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
- Rule-in (NT-proBNP): above 450 pg/mL under age 50, above 900 pg/mL at 50–74, above 1,800 pg/mL at 75 or older. Values in between need imaging and the clinical picture. ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
- False negatives: obesity. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- False positives: older age, atrial fibrillation, kidney disease, PE, sepsis. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- On sacubitril-valsartan use NT-proBNP, not BNP. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

#### Pulmonary embolism
- Estimate probability first ({{calc:wells-pe|Wells}}, revised {{calc:geneva-pe|Geneva}} or gestalt). It picks the next test. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Low probability and all {{calc:perc}} criteria met: no further testing. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Low or intermediate probability: D-dimer interpreted with {{calc:years-pe|YEARS}} or the {{calc:age-adjusted-d-dimer|age-adjusted cutoff}} (age × 10 µg/L FEU above age 50). A negative result excludes PE. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026, years-2017, adjust-pe-2014]
- High probability: CT pulmonary angiography directly. A D-dimer is not used. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Full workup, ventilation-perfusion scanning, pregnancy and inpatient caveats: [[pulm-vte-diagnosis]]. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]

#### Point-of-care ultrasound
- Use it in addition to the standard workup when the diagnosis is uncertain. It improves diagnostic accuracy but is not a replacement for the standard pathway (conditional recommendation, low-certainty evidence). ^[acp-pocus-2021]
- Lungs: ^[volpicelli-lus-2012]
  - Three or more B-lines per zone in two or more zones on each side indicate diffuse interstitial syndrome, in this setting most often cardiogenic edema (also ARDS, interstitial lung disease) ^[volpicelli-lus-2012]
  - Absent lung sliding suggests pneumothorax ^[volpicelli-lus-2012]
  - Consolidation with air bronchograms suggests pneumonia ^[volpicelli-lus-2012]
  - Anechoic pleural fluid ^[volpicelli-lus-2012]
- Heart: left ventricular function, right ventricular dilation, pericardial effusion. ^[common-practice]
- Veins: noncompressible proximal leg vein supports venous thromboembolism. ^[common-practice]
- Inferior vena cava: size and collapse estimate right atrial pressure but are unreliable alone. ^[common-practice]

#### CT chest
- CT pulmonary angiography for PE (above). Noncontrast or contrast chest CT when the radiograph and ultrasound do not explain hypoxemia, for suspected interstitial disease, mass, or complicated pneumonia. ^[common-practice]
- Not for the unstable patient before they are stabilized. ^[common-practice]

## Treatment
#### Initial support
- Oxygen to the target above. Avoid hyperoxia, which can worsen outcomes and hypercapnia in at-risk patients. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- Treat the cause found. This card does not repeat that care. ^[common-practice]

#### Treat the cause
- Pulmonary edema from heart failure: [[cardio-adhf]]. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Asthma and COPD: [[pulm-copd-exacerbation]]. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- PE: [[pulm-vte-diagnosis]]. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Pneumonia: [[id-cap]]. Sepsis: [[crit-sepsis]]. ^[ats-cap-2025]
- Acute coronary syndrome: [[cardio-chest-pain]]. ^[aha-acc-chest-pain-2021]
- Tension pneumothorax: needle or finger decompression, then chest tube. ^[common-practice]
- Metabolic acidosis: treat the underlying cause (for example [[endo-dka]]). ^[common-practice]

#### Noninvasive support
- Hypoxemic respiratory failure: high-flow nasal oxygen is preferred to conventional oxygen. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (pH ≤7.35 with PaCO₂ ≥45 mm Hg): noninvasive ventilation. ^[ats-nirs-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017, gold-copd-2026]
- Cardiogenic pulmonary edema with respiratory distress: noninvasive positive pressure (CPAP or bilevel), started early. ^[esc-hf-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017]
- Details, monitoring and failure criteria: [[crit-hypoxemic-respiratory-failure]]. ^[ats-nirs-2026]

## Monitor
- Reassess respiratory rate, SpO₂, work of breathing and mentation after each intervention and at least hourly in the first hours. ^[common-practice]
- Repeat a blood gas about 1 hour after starting noninvasive ventilation, after setting changes, or if mentation worsens. ^[bts-ics-hypercapnic-2016]
- Track {{calc:rox-index|ROX}} on high-flow oxygen. A low or falling value predicts failure. ^[roca-rox-2019]
- New or changing dyspnea after a diagnosis was made needs a fresh differential, not more of the same therapy. ^[common-practice]

## Escalation
- ICU: increasing oxygen needs, noninvasive ventilation, exhaustion, hypercapnia with acidosis, shock. ^[ats-nirs-2026, common-practice]
- Intubate for airway threat, respiratory arrest, inability to protect the airway, persistent hypoxemia or exhaustion despite noninvasive support, or worsening mentation. ^[ats-nirs-2026, common-practice]
- Monitor closely on noninvasive support and escalate promptly when it is failing. Delayed intubation may cause harm. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Anesthesia or airway team: predicted difficult airway, upper airway obstruction. ^[common-practice]
- PE with hypotension or shock: PE response team. See [[pulm-vte-diagnosis]]. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Pneumonia severity and site of care: {{calc:curb-65|CURB-65}}. See [[id-cap]]. ^[ats-cap-2025]

## More Information
### Special Populations: Older adults, obesity, chronic lung disease
- Older adults: ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
  - Atypical presentation and mixed causes ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
  - Natriuretic peptides rise with age, so use the age-specific NT-proBNP rule-in cutoffs ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
  - The rule-out cutoff is not age-adjusted ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
- Obesity lowers natriuretic peptide levels. Do not dismiss heart failure on a modestly raised value. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Chronic hypercapnia: aim for 88–92% and check a gas early. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]

### Evidence: Point-of-care ultrasound
- In a systematic review for the ACP guideline, adding ultrasound to the standard pathway raised the proportion of correct diagnoses from 59% to 91% (moderate certainty). Effects on mortality and time to treatment are unknown. ^[acp-pocus-2021]

## Pearls
- Treat first, then diagnose: oxygen to target and monitoring happen while you search. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- Always ask what cannot be missed: PE, pulmonary edema, tension pneumothorax, acute coronary syndrome, airway obstruction. ^[common-practice]
- A normal natriuretic peptide makes acute heart failure unlikely. A high one does not prove it. ^[esc-hf-2026, icon-reloaded-2018]
- Use {{calc:perc}} and D-dimer only when PE probability is low. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Do not give high oxygen targets to patients at risk of hypercapnia. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- Many patients have two causes at once. Finding one does not end the search. ^[common-practice]
