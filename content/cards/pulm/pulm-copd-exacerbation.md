## Do Now
1. Controlled oxygen
   - If: SpO₂ below 88% ^[bts-oxygen-2017, gold-copd-2026]
   - Check: blood gas; target SpO₂ 88–92% (pending gases, or after prior hypercapnic failure). ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
   - Start with a 24% or 28% Venturi mask or nasal cannula at 1–2 L/min. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
   - Repeat the blood gas 30–60 minutes after starting or changing oxygen. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
2. Short-acting bronchodilators
   - If: any exacerbation ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:albuterol-neb-copd}}

   {{dose:ipratropium-neb-copd}}

   - Air-driven nebulizer, with oxygen by nasal cannula during it. ^[gold-copd-2026, bts-oxygen-2017]
3. Systemic glucocorticoid
   - If: moderate or severe exacerbation ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:prednisone-copd}}

4. Antibiotic if indicated
   - If: purulent sputum plus more dyspnea or sputum volume, a prior positive sputum culture, or ventilatory support ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:amoxicillin-clavulanate-copd}}

   - Azithromycin is another first-line option; regimen in Treatment. ^[gold-copd-2026]
5. NIV for respiratory acidosis
   - If: PaCO₂ ≥45 mm Hg with pH ≤7.35, or severe dyspnea with respiratory muscle fatigue ^[gold-copd-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017]
   - Check: no immediate need for intubation (arrest, unable to protect airway, vomiting, refractory shock). ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
   - Blood gas 1 hour after starting NIV. ^[bts-ics-hypercapnic-2016]
6. Intubate if NIV fails
   - If: worsening pH, PaCO₂ or mental status on NIV, intolerance, or life-threatening hypoxemia ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - ICU; do not let NIV delay an indicated intubation. ^[gold-copd-2026, ats-nirs-2026]

:::note
- Pneumonia, heart failure, PE, myocardial infarction, arrhythmia and pneumothorax mimic or worsen exacerbations; look for them. ^[gold-copd-2026]
:::

## At a Glance
- Target SpO₂ 88–92% with controlled oxygen; check a blood gas. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, gold-copd-2026]
- Short-acting β₂-agonist, with or without ipratropium, then long-acting bronchodilators before discharge. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Systemic glucocorticoid for 5 days; oral works as well as IV. ^[gold-copd-2026, reduce-2013]
- Antibiotics for purulent sputum with another cardinal symptom, a prior positive sputum culture, or ventilatory support; 5–7 days. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Acute hypercapnic acidosis (pH ≤7.35): NIV first; it lowers mortality and intubation. ^[gold-copd-2026, ats-nirs-2026]
- Look for mimics: pneumonia, heart failure, PE, MI, arrhythmia, pneumothorax. ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Severity
:::scale Hospitalized severity
- No respiratory failure: RR ≤24, no accessory muscles, hypoxemia corrects with Venturi FiO₂ 24–35%, no rise in PaCO₂. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Respiratory failure: RR >24, accessory muscles, needs FiO₂ >35%, PaCO₂ 50–60 mm Hg or above baseline. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Ventilatory failure: acute mental status change, FiO₂ >40%, PaCO₂ >60 mm Hg, pH ≤7.25. ^[gold-copd-2026]
:::

{{criteria:copd-exacerbation-rome}}

{{criteria:copd-exacerbation-hospital}}

- Admit: respiratory failure signs (rising RR, accessory muscles, worsening gas exchange, mental status change), no response to initial treatment, serious comorbidity, insufficient home support. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- ICU: hypoxemia or hypercapnia worsening despite ventilatory support, worsening mental status, need for invasive ventilation, hemodynamic instability. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- NIV and high-flow oxygen belong where staff can monitor closely and intubate quickly (ICU or intermediate care). ^[ats-nirs-2026, gold-copd-2026]

## Causes
- **Infection:** [!most-common] respiratory viruses (rhinovirus, influenza, RSV, SARS-CoV-2); bacteria (*Haemophilus influenzae*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, *Streptococcus pneumoniae*). ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- **Pseudomonas risk:** frequent exacerbations, severe airflow obstruction, mechanical ventilation, prior isolation. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- **Environmental:** air pollution, smoke exposure. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- **Medications and adherence:** stopping maintenance inhalers, poor inhaler technique, sedatives or opioids (hypoventilation). ^[common-practice]
- **Mimics and coexisting conditions:** [!cannot-miss] pneumonia, decompensated heart failure, PE, myocardial infarction, arrhythmia (AF or flutter), pneumothorax. ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Presentation
- More dyspnea, cough, and sputum volume or purulence over days, often after a viral infection. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Tachypnea, tachycardia, wheeze, prolonged expiration, accessory muscle use, pursed lips. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- Respiratory failure: paradoxical abdominal motion, inability to speak in sentences, drowsiness or confusion (hypercapnia), cyanosis. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- Dyspnea without the other symptoms: think of another cause first. ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Diagnosis
#### Initial tests
- Blood gas: ABG in more severe cases, venous gas in milder ones; compare PaCO₂ and bicarbonate with baseline. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Chest radiograph: pneumonia, pneumothorax, heart failure. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- ECG and troponin; natriuretic peptide for heart failure. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- CBC (eosinophils), BMP, CRP. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- Respiratory viral testing; sputum culture with frequent exacerbations, severe airflow obstruction or ventilation. ^[gold-copd-2026]

#### Interpretation
- Acute hypercapnia: pH ≤7.35 with PaCO₂ ≥45 mm Hg; a normal pH with high bicarbonate means chronic retention. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- Pulse oximetry is less accurate in darker skin; confirm with a blood gas when it matters. ^[gold-copd-2026, sjoding-oximetry-2020]

#### Further testing
- PE: clinical probability, D-dimer, CT pulmonary angiography when no clear trigger or with PE features ([[pulm-vte-diagnosis|VTE diagnosis]]). ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Echocardiogram or CT chest by the leading alternative diagnosis. ^[gold-copd-2026]

#### Avoid
- Spirometry during the exacerbation. ^[common-practice]
- Procalcitonin to decide on antibiotics (conflicting results; higher mortality in one ICU study). ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Treatment
1. Oxygen and ventilatory support
   - Controlled oxygen to SpO₂ 88–92%; high-concentration oxygen raised mortality in a prehospital trial. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, austin-oxygen-2010]
   - NIV via facemask for acute hypercapnic respiratory failure (pH ≤7.35, PaCO₂ ≥45 mm Hg), severe dyspnea with fatigue, or persistent hypoxemia. ^[ats-nirs-2026, gold-copd-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017]
   - High-flow nasal oxygen: hypoxemia despite conventional oxygen; an alternative to NIV only with mild acidemia (pH >7.25) and NIV promptly available; about a third switch to NIV. ^[ats-nirs-2026, gold-copd-2026]
   - NIV preferred over high-flow for hypercapnic failure; high-flow if NIV is not tolerated. ^[ers-hfnc-2022]
   - Stop NIV once the patient tolerates at least 4 hours unassisted; no weaning needed. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - Invasive ventilation: NIV failure, intolerance of high-flow or NIV, arrest, worsening consciousness or uncontrolled agitation, massive aspiration or persistent vomiting, inability to clear secretions, refractory shock, severe arrhythmias. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - Settings and devices: [[crit-hypoxemic-respiratory-failure/treatment|hypoxemic respiratory failure]]. ^[common-practice]

2. Bronchodilators
   - Short-acting β₂-agonist with or without short-acting antimuscarinic; nebulizer or inhaler with spacer. ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:albuterol-neb-copd}}

   {{dose:ipratropium-neb-copd}}

   - Continue or start long-acting bronchodilators as soon as possible before discharge. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - No IV methylxanthines (theophylline, aminophylline). ^[gold-copd-2026]

3. Systemic glucocorticoids
   - Moderate or severe exacerbations; shorten recovery, improve FEV₁ and oxygenation, reduce relapse and length of stay. ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:prednisone-copd}}

   - 5 days is as effective as 14. ^[reduce-2013]
   - Nebulized budesonide is an alternative in some patients (off-label in adults). ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - Eosinophil-guided steroids are safe in primary care; not yet validated in hospitalized patients. ^[gold-copd-2026, starr2-2024]

4. Antibiotics
   - Give when at least two of more dyspnea, fever, more sputum and purulent sputum (purulence one of them), a prior positive sputum culture, or any ventilatory support. ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{regimen:copd-exacerbation}}

   - Influenza: oseltamivir for hospitalized patients. ^[idsa-influenza-2018]

   {{dose:oseltamivir-influenza}}

   - Pneumonia on imaging: treat as [[id-cap|community-acquired pneumonia]]. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]

5. Supportive care
   - VTE prophylaxis (up to 5.9% of hospitalized patients have PE). ^[gold-copd-2026]

   {{dose:enoxaparin-prophylaxis}}

   - Fluid balance, diuretics when indicated, comorbidities, nutrition. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - Smoking cessation at every contact. ^[gold-copd-2026]
   - No respiratory stimulants. ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Monitor
:::times Blood gas, minutes after starting or changing oxygen ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- 30
- 60
:::

- SpO₂ continuously while on oxygen; keep 88–92%. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- On NIV: pH, PaCO₂, RR and mental status; blood gas 1 hour after starting and after changes. ^[bts-ics-hypercapnic-2016]
- High-flow in hypercapnia: close observation and a clear path to NIV. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Glucose on steroids, especially in diabetes. ^[common-practice]
- Expected recovery takes up to 4–6 weeks; some never return to baseline. ^[gold-copd-2026]

## Escalation
- ICU or intermediate care: NIV, high-flow with rising needs, worsening gas exchange or mental status, hemodynamic instability. ^[gold-copd-2026, ats-nirs-2026]
- Pulmonology: frequent exacerbations, unclear diagnosis, home NIV or oxygen decisions. ^[common-practice]
- Palliative care: very severe COPD, recurrent ventilation, patient wishes; ask about advance directives early. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: Oxygen target, steroid dose
:::compare Oxygen target with hypercapnia risk
- BTS 2017: 88–92% for COPD and others at risk of hypercapnia; 94–98% for most other acutely ill adults. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- GOLD 2026: controlled oxygen for hypoxemia; no numeric target in the exacerbation chapter. ^[gold-copd-2026]
:::

:::compare Systemic steroid
- GOLD 2026: prednisone-equivalent, 5 days; eosinophil guidance not yet for inpatients. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- VA/DoD 2026 (outpatients only): selective steroids by severity and eosinophils, most benefit at ≥300 cells/µL. ^[vadod-copd-2026]
:::

### Special Populations: Diabetes, heart failure, pneumonia
- Diabetes: steroid-induced hyperglycemia; adjust insulin ([[endo-inpatient-hyperglycemia|inpatient hyperglycemia]]). ^[common-practice]
- Heart failure: β₁-selective β-blockers may be continued. ^[common-practice]
- Pneumonia coexisting: treat both; even short steroid bursts raise pneumonia and sepsis risk. ^[gold-copd-2026]

### Complications: Respiratory and cardiac
- Acute hypercapnic respiratory failure, need for invasive ventilation. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Cardiovascular events (MI, heart failure, arrhythmia, stroke): risk highest in the first 30 days, elevated for up to 1–2 years. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Readmission in 30–50% within 30 days. ^[gold-copd-2026]

### After the Acute Phase: Discharge and prevention
- Discharge on long-acting bronchodilators; add an inhaled corticosteroid to LABA+LAMA with ≥1 exacerbation and high blood eosinophils. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Check inhaler technique and adherence; education and an action plan. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Pulmonary rehabilitation within 4 weeks of discharge. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Follow-up within 1 month, and at 3 months with spirometry and reassessment of long-term oxygen. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Vaccines: influenza, pneumococcal, RSV, COVID-19; smoking cessation. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- CT chest for recurrent exacerbations (bronchiectasis, emphysema). ^[gold-copd-2026]

### Evidence: Key trials
- REDUCE (2013): 5 days of prednisone was noninferior to 14 days for re-exacerbation. ^[reduce-2013]
- Austin (2010): titrated oxygen to 88–92% lowered prehospital mortality versus high-flow oxygen. ^[austin-oxygen-2010]
- CORTICO-COP (2019): eosinophil-guided steroids in hospitalized patients were noninferior and cut steroid exposure. ^[cortico-cop-2019]
- STARR2 (2024): eosinophil-guided prednisolone was noninferior in primary care. ^[starr2-2024]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology: Hyperinflation
- More than 80% of exacerbations are managed as outpatients; after hospitalization, 5-year mortality is about 50%. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- Airway inflammation, mucus and air trapping raise work of breathing; respiratory muscle fatigue leads to hypercapnia. ^[gold-copd-2026, common-practice]
- Excess oxygen worsens hypercapnia by releasing hypoxic vasoconstriction (more dead space) and the Haldane effect. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, common-practice]

## Pearls
- A normal SpO₂ on high-flow oxygen can hide a rising PaCO₂; aim for 88–92%. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- NIV should not delay an indicated intubation. ^[gold-copd-2026, ats-nirs-2026]
- Five days of steroids; no taper. ^[reduce-2013, common-practice]
- Exacerbation without purulent sputum usually needs no antibiotic. ^[gold-copd-2026]
- No clear trigger and not improving: think PE, heart failure, pneumothorax. ^[gold-copd-2026]
