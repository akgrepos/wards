## At a Glance
- Third-generation cephalosporin: once-daily IV or IM, with good CNS penetration. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Covers streptococci, methicillin-susceptible *Staphylococcus aureus*, *Haemophilus*, *Neisseria* and most Enterobacterales. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Gaps: MRSA, *Enterococcus*, *Listeria* and *Pseudomonas* (not reliable). No activity against *Chlamydia trachomatis*. ^[common-practice, label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- No dose change for kidney or liver impairment alone. The maximum drops when liver dysfunction and significant kidney disease coexist. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Never mix with calcium-containing IV solutions in the same line (precipitation). Contraindicated in neonates who need IV calcium. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- A penicillin allergy label alone rarely rules it out. Check the reaction history, not the label. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]

## Dose Adjustment
- Kidney impairment: ceftriaxone leaves by both the kidney and the bile, so usual doses need no change, including in dialysis. Neurotoxicity still occurs in severe kidney impairment (see Adverse Effects). ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Liver dysfunction alone: no change. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Liver dysfunction plus significant kidney disease: the daily maximum is lowered. It is on the first dose row's sheet. Monitor closely for safety and efficacy. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis do not remove it. No supplemental dose after dialysis. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Older adults: no change for age alone at usual doses unless severe kidney and liver disease coexist. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

## Administration
- IV: infuse over 30 minutes. IM is an option for gonorrhea and other single doses. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Diluents: use 0.9% sodium chloride or 5% dextrose. Never reconstitute or dilute with a calcium-containing fluid such as lactated Ringer's. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Calcium-containing IV products (including IV calcium gluconate or chloride and parenteral nutrition): ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - Never infuse in the same line at the same time, including at a Y-site. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - In patients other than neonates, they may be given one after the other if the line is flushed thoroughly with a compatible fluid in between. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Oral calcium and IM ceftriaxone with any calcium product: no interaction has been reported. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Incompatible in the same line: vancomycin, aminoglycosides, fluconazole and amsacrine. Give one after the other and flush between. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- IM injection: lidocaine as the solvent is allowed only for IM use. IV solutions containing lidocaine are contraindicated. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

## Contraindications and Boxed Warnings
- No boxed warning. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Contraindications: ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - Hypersensitivity to ceftriaxone, any excipient or any other cephalosporin. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - Premature neonates up to a postmenstrual age of 41 weeks, and hyperbilirubinemic neonates (displaces bilirubin from albumin). ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - Neonates up to 28 days who need or may need calcium-containing IV solutions. Fatal crystal deposition in the lungs and kidneys has occurred. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
  - IV solutions containing lidocaine. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Penicillin allergy label, no history of anaphylaxis: give ceftriaxone without testing. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
- Penicillin anaphylaxis: a cephalosporin with a different R1 side chain, such as ceftriaxone, can be given without testing. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
- Prior reaction to a cephalosporin: ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
  - Avoid agents that share its R1 side chain. Ceftriaxone shares it with cefotaxime, cefpodoxime, cefditoren and ceftizoxime. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
  - Non-anaphylactic reaction: a direct challenge with a different R1 cephalosporin is reasonable. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
  - Anaphylaxis: skin testing first. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
- Severe delayed reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS) to a β-lactam: avoid all β-lactams. The no-testing lines above do not apply. ^[aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]
- The label says patients with a previous reaction to penicillin may be at greater risk. Use the allergy history above to decide, not the label alone. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026, aaaai-drug-allergy-2022]

## Adverse Effects
#### Common
- Eosinophilia (6%), thrombocytosis (5.1%), diarrhea or loose stools (2.7%), leukopenia (2.1%), rash (1.7%), raised AST or ALT (about 3%). ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

#### Uncommon
- Injection-site pain and phlebitis (<1%). ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

#### Serious
- Anaphylaxis, severe cutaneous reactions (Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, AGEP). ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- *Clostridioides difficile* infection, which can appear more than 2 months after the last dose. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Immune-mediated hemolytic anemia, sometimes fatal. Stop ceftriaxone until the cause is clear. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Biliary sludge and pseudolithiasis from ceftriaxone-calcium precipitate: reversible, most often in children. Stop if gallbladder symptoms or typical ultrasound findings develop. It can look like gallstones on ultrasound. Pancreatitis from biliary obstruction has also been reported. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Urolithiasis and post-renal acute kidney injury from precipitate in the urinary tract. Keep patients hydrated. Stop for oliguria or flank pain. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Encephalopathy, seizures, myoclonus and nonconvulsive status epilepticus, especially in severe kidney impairment. They occasionally occur despite correct dosing. They reverse after stopping. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Prolonged prothrombin time and bleeding, mainly with low vitamin K stores. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Agranulocytosis after prolonged courses. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

## Interactions
- **Calcium-containing IV solutions:** precipitation in the line or the body, most dangerous in neonates. See Administration. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- **Vitamin K antagonists:** more bleeding risk. Check the INR often during and after treatment and adjust warfarin. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- **Chloramphenicol:** antagonism seen in vitro. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- **Probenecid:** no change in elimination. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- **Lab tests:** the direct Coombs test can turn positive, and urine glucose by non-enzymatic methods can read falsely high. Some glucose meters can read falsely low, so check the meter's instructions. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Check other combinations with the Check Interactions link on a dose sheet. ^[common-practice]

| What | When | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Diarrhea | Daily, and for weeks to months after the course | Test for *C. difficile*, and stop other unneeded antibiotics ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026] |
| Neurologic status | Daily in severe kidney impairment | New confusion, myoclonus or seizure: stop ceftriaxone ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026] |

## More Information
### Pregnancy and Lactation: Use when needed
- Pregnancy: no harm in animal studies (mice, rats, primates). No adequate controlled human studies, so use it when clearly needed. It is the recommended agent for gonorrhea in pregnancy. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026, cdc-sti-2021]
- Lactation: low milk levels, not expected to harm the breastfed infant. Acceptable while breastfeeding. Watch the infant for diarrhea or thrush. ^[lactmed-ceftriaxone-2024]

### Overdose and Reversal: No antidote
- Overdose has caused encephalopathy and seizures in severe kidney impairment. Stop the drug and give supportive care. Dialysis does not remove it, and there is no specific antidote. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

### Evidence: Where the doses come from
- Doses come from the FDA label and the newest US guidelines for each indication. Mixed indications use one dose row. Check each row's sheet for its sources. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]

## Pearls
- Calcium and ceftriaxone: flush the line, use a separate line, or stagger the doses. Never one line, and never in neonates. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
- Do not rely on ceftriaxone for *Listeria*, *Enterococcus* or MRSA. Empiric meningitis therapy adds vancomycin, and ampicillin over age 50 for *Listeria*. ^[idsa-meningitis-2004, common-practice]
- Ceftriaxone reaches the CNS well, which is why it is a standard meningitis agent. ^[idsa-meningitis-2004]
- Suspect biliary sludge in a patient on ceftriaxone with new right-upper-quadrant pain. ^[label-ceftriaxone-2026]
