## At a Glance
- Clinical diagnosis. About 1 in 3 leg cellulitis admissions is a mimic (bilateral leg redness is rarely cellulitis). ^[weng-misdiagnosed-cellulitis-2017, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Nonpurulent cellulitis is mostly β-hemolytic streptococci: a β-lactam alone. Add MRSA coverage only with specific risks. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Purulent infection (abscess): drainage first. Antibiotics against MRSA for systemic signs. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- 5 days of therapy, longer only if not improved. Elevate the limb and treat toe-web disease and edema. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Pain out of proportion, bullae, crepitus, skin necrosis or rapid spread: suspect necrotizing infection and call surgery now. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]

## Severity
:::scale Nonpurulent cellulitis
- Mild: no systemic signs of infection ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Moderate: systemic signs of infection ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Severe: failed oral antibiotics, {{calc:sirs}}, immunocompromise, or signs of deeper infection (bullae, skin sloughing, hypotension, organ dysfunction) ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
:::

- Systemic signs: temperature >38 °C (100.4 °F) or <36 °C (96.8 °F), heart rate >90 beats/min, respiratory rate >24 breaths/min, or WBC >12,000 or <4,000 cells/µL. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- IDSA puts {{calc:sirs}} in the severe tier, where an agent covering MRSA and streptococci is recommended. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Outpatient treatment without {{calc:sirs}}, altered mental status or hemodynamic instability. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Admit: concern for deeper or necrotizing infection, severe immunocompromise, poor adherence, or failure of outpatient therapy. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]

## Causes
- **β-hemolytic streptococci:** [!most-common] group A most often, also groups B, C and G. The usual cause of nonpurulent cellulitis and erysipelas. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- ***Staphylococcus aureus*, including MRSA:** purulent infection, abscess, penetrating trauma, injection drug use. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- **Exposures:** animal bites (*Pasteurella*, *Capnocytophaga*), fresh water (*Aeromonas*), salt water (*Vibrio vulnificus*, especially with cirrhosis). ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- **Immunocompromised hosts:** gram-negative bacilli, fungi and mycobacteria are possible. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- **Necrotizing fasciitis:** [!cannot-miss] polymicrobial (mixed aerobes and anaerobes) or monomicrobial (group A streptococcus, less often MRSA, *Vibrio*, *Aeromonas*). *Clostridium* causes gas gangrene. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- **Risk factors:** lymphedema or chronic edema, venous insufficiency, toe-web fissuring or tinea, obesity, prior cellulitis, skin breaks. ^[idsa-ssti-2014, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- **Mimics:** stasis dermatitis, lipodermatosclerosis, contact dermatitis, lymphedema, gout, DVT, superficial thrombophlebitis, panniculitis, drug eruption. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]

## Presentation
- Cellulitis: spreading erythema, warmth, edema and tenderness with indistinct borders. Usually one lower leg. ^[idsa-ssti-2014, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Erysipelas: raised, sharply demarcated, bright red plaque. Legs or face. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Lymphangitic streaking, tender regional nodes ^[idsa-ssti-2014, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Vesicles or bullae ^[idsa-ssti-2014, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Fever may precede the rash ^[idsa-ssti-2014, raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Bilateral, painless, chronic or itchy: think of a mimic first. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Necrotizing infection red flags: pain out of proportion, woody induration, violaceous bullae, skin anesthesia, crepitus, gas, rapid progression, systemic toxicity. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]

## Diagnosis
- Clinical. Examine the toe webs for fissures, scaling or maceration. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Blood and skin cultures not routine. Obtain blood cultures with severe systemic features (high fever, hypotension), malignancy on chemotherapy, neutropenia, severe cell-mediated immunodeficiency, immersion injury or animal bite. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Purulent: culture the drained pus. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Point-of-care ultrasound to find an occult abscess. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Necrotizing infection: imaging must not delay surgical exploration. A {{calc:lrinec}} score ≥6 has a sensitivity of only about 68%. ^[idsa-ssti-2014, fernando-nsti-2019]
- Unclear diagnosis or no response: dermatology review. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]

## Monitor
- Mark the border and photograph daily. ^[common-practice]
- Erythema and systemic signs may worsen at first on effective therapy. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- No improvement after 48–72 hours, or spread: reconsider abscess, MRSA, a mimic, necrotizing infection or deeper infection. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016, common-practice]
- Switch IV to oral when afebrile and improving. ^[common-practice]

## Escalation
- Surgery, immediately: suspected necrotizing infection or gas gangrene. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- ICU: septic shock or toxic shock syndrome. ^[common-practice]
- Infectious diseases: immunocompromise, unusual exposure, failure to improve. ^[common-practice]
- Dermatology: diagnosis in doubt, bilateral or recurrent disease. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: Adjunctive corticosteroids
:::compare Corticosteroids with antibiotics
- IDSA 2014: May be considered in nondiabetic adults (weak recommendation, one trial). ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Meta-analysis 2024: Too little data to judge corticosteroids. NSAIDs sped early response (day 3) only. ^[hamill-antiinflammatory-2024]
:::

### Special Populations: Immunocompromise, bites, diabetes
- Neutropenia or severe immunodeficiency: broad empiric therapy, blood cultures, early biopsy or aspirate. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Animal bites: amoxicillin-clavulanate. Outside the scope of this card. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Diabetic foot infection and surgical site infection have separate guidelines. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]

### Complications: Abscess, necrotizing infection
- Abscess, bacteremia, necrotizing fasciitis, toxic shock syndrome, osteomyelitis or septic arthritis. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Recurrence: common with lymphedema and venous disease. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]

### After the Acute Phase: Recurrence prevention
- Treat predisposing conditions: edema, obesity, eczema, venous insufficiency, toe-web abnormalities. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Leg edema: compression stockings reduced recurrence. ^[webb-compression-2020]
- 3–4 episodes a year despite this: consider prophylactic penicillin. The benefit fades after it stops. ^[idsa-ssti-2014, patch1-2013]

{{dose:penicillin-vk-prophylaxis}}

### Evidence: Key trials
- 5 days was as effective as 10 days for uncomplicated cellulitis improving at day 5. ^[hepburn-cellulitis-2004]
- PATCH I (2013): penicillin for 12 months after recurrent leg cellulitis reduced recurrence during prophylaxis. The effect waned after it stopped. ^[patch1-2013]
- Compression (2020): compression therapy for chronic leg edema reduced recurrent cellulitis. ^[webb-compression-2020]

## Pearls
- Bilateral leg "cellulitis" is usually stasis dermatitis or lymphedema. ^[raff-cellulitis-2016]
- Worsening redness on day 1 of the right antibiotic is common. Reassess before broadening. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Pure cellulitis without purulence does not need MRSA coverage unless a specific risk is present. ^[idsa-ssti-2014]
- Normal {{calc:lrinec}} or no gas on imaging does not exclude necrotizing fasciitis. ^[fernando-nsti-2019]
