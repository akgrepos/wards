## Do Now
1. Secure airway and access
   - If: shock, altered mental status or ongoing hematemesis ^[common-practice]
   - Check: Airway protection before endoscopy if mental status is depressed or hematemesis is brisk. ^[common-practice]
   - Two large-bore IVs. ^[common-practice]
   - Type and crossmatch. ^[common-practice]
   - CBC, chemistry with BUN and creatinine, INR, liver tests. ^[common-practice]
2. Resuscitate
   - Hemorrhage: blood products early. Crystalloid only as a small-volume bridge until blood is available. ^[common-practice]
   - Transfuse red cells at {{threshold:ugib-hb-restrictive}}, or {{threshold:ugib-hb-cardiovascular}} with preexisting cardiovascular disease. ^[acg-ugib-2021, aabb-rbc-2023]
   - Hypotensive or exsanguinating bleeding: transfuse on clinical grounds, before the hemoglobin reaches the threshold. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
3. Give erythromycin before endoscopy
   - Check: {{calc:qtc}} and interacting drugs. ^[label-erythrocin-iv-2025]

   {{dose:erythromycin-iv-ugib}}

4. Start high-dose IV PPI
   - Consider before endoscopy. Never delay endoscopy for it. ^[esge-pub-2026]

   {{dose:pantoprazole-iv-ugib-infusion}}

5. Hold bleeding drugs
   - Stop NSAIDs. ^[common-practice]
   - Hold anticoagulants and P2Y12 inhibitors pending endoscopy, with cardiology input for recent stents or acute coronary syndrome. ^[common-practice]
   - Aspirin for secondary cardiovascular prevention: do not hold. If held, restart the day endoscopic hemostasis is confirmed. ^[acg-cag-antithrombotic-2022]
   - Warfarin needing reversal (life-threatening bleeding or markedly supratherapeutic INR): prothrombin complex concentrate rather than fresh frozen plasma. Other reversal agents are on a later card. ^[acg-cag-antithrombotic-2022]
6. Call gastroenterology
   - Endoscopy within 24 hours of presentation, after resuscitation. Emergent endoscopy (within 6 hours) only if still unstable despite resuscitation. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Unstable, or ongoing bleeding: ICU or step-down bed. ^[common-practice]

:::note
- Cirrhosis or stigmata of liver disease: treat as variceal bleeding until proven otherwise (vasoactive drug such as octreotide and IV antibiotic prophylaxis at once, endoscopy within 12 hours). A full variceal card comes later. ^[aasld-portal-htn-2024]
:::

## At a Glance
- Resuscitate first, then risk-stratify. Endoscopy within 24 hours of presentation after resuscitation. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
- {{calc:gbs}} {{threshold:gbs-very-low-risk}}: very low risk. May be discharged with outpatient follow-up. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
- Restrictive transfusion: {{threshold:ugib-hb-restrictive}}. {{threshold:ugib-hb-cardiovascular}} with preexisting cardiovascular disease. ^[acg-ugib-2021, aabb-rbc-2023]
- Erythromycin before endoscopy. High-dose IV PPI for 72 hours after endoscopic therapy of high-risk ulcers. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
- Endoscopic therapy for {{criteria:forrest}} Ia, Ib and IIa ulcers. Rebleeding: repeat endoscopy, then embolization or surgery. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
- Variceal bleeding is a different pathway. ^[aasld-portal-htn-2024]

## Severity
- Risk stratification: {{calc:gbs}} (very low risk at {{threshold:gbs-very-low-risk}}). ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
- {{calc:aims65}} predicts in-hospital mortality. Not suggested for identifying very-low-risk patients for discharge. ^[icg-ugib-2019]
- Shock: hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status, poor perfusion. Resuscitate before scoring. ^[common-practice]
- Admit unless very low risk. Unstable or actively bleeding: ICU or step-down. ^[acg-ugib-2021, common-practice]

{{criteria:forrest}}

## Causes
- **Ulcer disease:** [!most-common] duodenal and gastric ulcers, from *H. pylori*, NSAIDs, aspirin and other antiplatelets, anticoagulants, critical illness. ^[acg-ugib-2021, common-practice]
- **Mucosal:** erosive esophagitis, gastritis and duodenitis. ^[common-practice]
- **Mallory-Weiss tear:** after retching or vomiting. ^[common-practice]
- **Vascular:** angiodysplasia, Dieulafoy lesion, gastric antral vascular ectasia. ^[common-practice]
- **Neoplasm:** gastric, esophageal or duodenal cancer. ^[common-practice]
- **Aortoenteric fistula:** [!cannot-miss] prior aortic graft. Massive or herald bleeding. ^[common-practice]
- **Varices and portal hypertensive gastropathy:** separate pathway when cirrhosis is known or suspected. ^[aasld-portal-htn-2024]

## Presentation
- Hematemesis, coffee-ground emesis, melena. ^[common-practice]
- Hematochezia with shock: a brisk upper source is possible. ^[common-practice]
- Lightheadedness, syncope, tachycardia, hypotension, angina or dyspnea from anemia. ^[common-practice]
- Older or cardiac patients may present with ischemia rather than overt bleeding. ^[common-practice]

## Diagnosis
- Initial tests: CBC (hemoglobin lags behind acute loss), BUN and creatinine, INR, liver tests, type and crossmatch. ECG and troponin if cardiac risk. ^[common-practice]
- Score the patient with {{calc:gbs}} and {{calc:aims65}}. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
- Upper endoscopy is diagnostic and therapeutic. Classify the stigmata with {{criteria:forrest}}. ^[acg-ugib-2021]
- *H. pylori*: test every bleeding ulcer. A negative test during the acute bleed or on a PPI can be falsely negative, so retest later. ^[common-practice]
- Video capsule endoscopy and blood-sensing capsules are not recommended before endoscopy. ^[esge-pub-2026]

## Treatment
1. Resuscitation
   - Two large-bore IVs, airway protection if needed, early blood products for hemorrhage. Crystalloid only as a small-volume bridge until blood is available. ^[common-practice]
   - Transfuse at {{threshold:ugib-hb-restrictive}}. {{threshold:ugib-hb-cardiovascular}} with preexisting cardiovascular disease. ^[acg-ugib-2021, aabb-rbc-2023]
   - Hypotensive or exsanguinating bleeding: these thresholds do not apply. Transfuse on clinical grounds. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
2. Coagulopathy and platelets
   - Warfarin needing reversal: prothrombin complex concentrate rather than fresh frozen plasma. Routine vitamin K is suggested against (consider it when prolonged reversal is intended). ^[acg-cag-antithrombotic-2022]
   - Antiplatelet effect alone: platelet transfusion is not suggested. ^[acg-cag-antithrombotic-2022]
   - Active bleeding with thrombocytopenia: platelet transfusion is common practice. ^[common-practice]
   - Reversal of direct oral anticoagulants and heparin is on a later card. See [[heme-anticoagulation-management|Anticoagulant Selection]]. ^[common-practice]
3. Before endoscopy
   - Erythromycin is suggested for all patients with UGIB before endoscopy. ^[acg-ugib-2021]

   {{dose:erythromycin-iv-ugib}}

   - High-dose IV PPI may be considered. Do not delay endoscopy. ACG 2021 makes no recommendation for or against. ^[esge-pub-2026, acg-ugib-2021]

   {{dose:pantoprazole-iv-ugib-infusion}}

   - Metoclopramide only if IV erythromycin is unavailable. ^[esge-pub-2026]
4. Endoscopy and hemostasis
   - Timing: within 24 hours of presentation, after resuscitation. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Emergent (6 hours) or urgent (12 hours) endoscopy is not recommended unless unstable despite adequate resuscitation. ^[esge-pub-2026]
   - Therapy for active spurting or oozing and nonbleeding visible vessels ({{criteria:forrest}} Ia, Ib, IIa). ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Methods: bipolar or heater-probe thermal therapy, absolute ethanol injection, clips. Epinephrine injection only combined with a second method. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Adherent clot: remove it and treat what lies beneath if safely possible. ^[esge-pub-2026]
5. After endoscopy
   - High-risk stigmata treated endoscopically: high-dose PPI for 72 hours (continuous infusion, or intermittent per ACG), then twice-daily oral PPI to day 14, then once daily. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]

   {{dose:pantoprazole-iv-ugib-infusion}}
   {{dose:pantoprazole-iv-ugib-intermittent}}
   {{dose:esomeprazole-iv-ugib}}
   {{dose:pantoprazole-po-ugib}}

   - Low-risk stigmata (flat spot, clean base): once-daily oral PPI. Early feeding. Prompt discharge may be considered. ^[acg-ugib-2021, icg-ugib-2019]
   - Early oral nutrition within 24 hours of durable hemostasis. ^[esge-pub-2026]
6. Rebleeding or failed endoscopic therapy
   - Repeat endoscopy. A second attempt with an over-the-scope clip may be considered. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Persistent bleeding after repeat endoscopy: transcatheter angiographic embolization. ^[acg-ugib-2021, esge-pub-2026]
   - Surgery when embolization is unavailable or fails. ^[esge-pub-2026]
   - Selected high-risk ulcers (instability at presentation, posterior duodenal wall, larger than 2 cm, uncertain hemostasis): prophylactic embolization may be considered. ^[esge-pub-2026]
7. Prevent recurrence
   - *H. pylori* positive: treat and confirm eradication. Regimens come with the peptic ulcer card. ^[acg-hpylori-2024]
   - Stop NSAIDs. If aspirin or another antithrombotic must continue, keep a PPI with it. ^[icg-ugib-2019]

## Monitor
- Vital signs and urine output. Serial hemoglobin until stable. ^[common-practice]
- Rebleeding: recurrent hematemesis or melena, hemodynamic instability, falling hemoglobin. ^[common-practice]
- Check {{calc:qtc}} before and after erythromycin if other QT-prolonging drugs are given. ^[label-erythrocin-iv-2025]

## Escalation
- Gastroenterology for every patient with suspected upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Endoscopy within 24 hours. ^[acg-ugib-2021]
- ICU or step-down: shock, ongoing bleeding, airway concern, massive transfusion. ^[common-practice]
- Interventional radiology: bleeding refractory to endoscopic therapy. ^[esge-pub-2026]
- Surgery: failed embolization or none locally. ^[esge-pub-2026]
- Hepatology: known or suspected cirrhosis (variceal pathway). ^[aasld-portal-htn-2024]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: Endoscopic hemostasis tools
:::compare Hemostatic powder and clips
- ACG 2021: Hemostatic powder is suggested for actively bleeding ulcers. Over-the-scope clips for recurrent bleeding. ^[acg-ugib-2021]
- ESGE 2026: Hemostatic agents are not first-line monotherapy. Over-the-scope clips may replace combination therapy as first-line. ^[esge-pub-2026]
:::

### Special Populations: Antithrombotic agents
- Aspirin for secondary prevention: continue, or restart the day hemostasis is confirmed. Aspirin for primary prevention: hold and reassess. Little benefit and not advised with high bleeding risk. ^[acg-cag-antithrombotic-2022]
- Anticoagulation: resume as soon as clinically indicated, weighing thromboembolic risk. ^[esge-pub-2026]
- Recent stent or acute coronary syndrome on dual antiplatelet therapy: involve cardiology before stopping. ^[common-practice]

### After the Acute Phase: Discharge and prevention
- Iron therapy before discharge if iron deficient or anemic. ^[esge-pub-2026]
- Treat *H. pylori* and confirm eradication. ^[acg-hpylori-2024]
- Avoid NSAIDs. PPI while on aspirin, other antiplatelets or anticoagulants. ^[icg-ugib-2019]
- Gastric ulcer: repeat endoscopy to confirm healing and exclude malignancy is common practice. ^[common-practice]

## Pearls
- Resuscitate before scoring. Transfuse unstable patients without waiting for the hemoglobin. ^[common-practice]
- Erythromycin and a PPI never delay endoscopy. ^[esge-pub-2026]
- The US label does not establish pantoprazole for upper gastrointestinal bleeding. Esomeprazole is the labeled IV regimen after therapeutic endoscopy. ^[label-pantoprazole-iv-2026, label-esomeprazole-iv-2025]
- Cirrhosis changes everything: variceal pathway. ^[aasld-portal-htn-2024]
