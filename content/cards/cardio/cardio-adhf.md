## At a Glance
- Classify by congestion (wet or dry) and perfusion (warm or cold). Most are warm and wet. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Find the trigger: ischemia, arrhythmia, hypertension, infection, nonadherence to medications or sodium restriction, offending drugs. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Congestion: prompt IV loop diuretic, dose at least equal to the home oral daily dose. Check response within hours. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- Poor response: double the loop dose, then add a thiazide-type diuretic or acetazolamide. ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019, advor-2022]
- Hypoperfusion or hypotension: ICU and cardiology for inotropes or vasopressors. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- GDMT and discharge: ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, strong-hf-2022]
  - Continue GDMT unless unstable. Start missing pillars before discharge.
  - Decongest fully. Follow up within 7 days.

## Severity
#### Hemodynamic profile
- **Warm and wet:** ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
  - Findings: congestion, adequate perfusion (most common)
  - Approach: diuretics. Vasodilator if hypertensive
- **Cold and wet:** ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
  - Findings: congestion with hypoperfusion: cool extremities, narrow pulse pressure, altered mentation, oliguria, rising lactate
  - Approach: ICU. Inotropes, vasopressors if hypotensive.
  - Diuretics once perfused
- **Cold and dry:** ^[esc-hf-2026]
  - Findings: hypoperfusion without congestion
  - Approach: cautious fluid if preload-dependent. Inotropes
- **Warm and dry:** ^[esc-hf-2026]
  - Findings: compensated
  - Approach: look for another cause of symptoms

- Cardiogenic shock (hypotension with hypoperfusion): ICU and cardiology emergently. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Respiratory distress or SpO₂ <90% despite oxygen: noninvasive ventilation, ICU if not improving. ^[esc-hf-2026]
- Higher risk: SBP <100 mm Hg, high BUN, rising creatinine, hyponatremia, high natriuretic peptide or troponin. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]

## Causes
- **Ischemic:** [!cannot-miss] acute coronary syndrome. Mechanical complications of MI. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- **Arrhythmia:** AF with RVR, other tachyarrhythmias, bradyarrhythmia. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- **Hemodynamic:** uncontrolled hypertension, acute valve dysfunction (endocarditis, chordal rupture, prosthetic valve thrombosis), PE. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- **Nonadherence:** medications, sodium or fluid intake. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- **Drugs and toxins:** NSAIDs, nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers in HFrEF, thiazolidinediones, some antiarrhythmics and chemotherapy, alcohol, stimulants. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- **Systemic:** infection, anemia, thyroid disease, worsening kidney function, pregnancy. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- **Mimics:** pneumonia, COPD exacerbation, PE, nephrotic syndrome, cirrhosis, ARDS. ^[common-practice]

## Presentation
- Dyspnea on exertion, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, edema, weight gain, abdominal fullness. Fatigue in low output. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Congestion: elevated JVP, hepatojugular reflux, crackles, S3, peripheral edema, ascites, pleural effusions. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Elevated JVP and orthopnea are the most useful bedside signs of high filling pressures. Crackles may be absent in chronic heart failure. ^[common-practice]
- Hypoperfusion: cool extremities, narrow pulse pressure, confusion, oliguria. ^[esc-hf-2026]

## Diagnosis
#### Initial tests
- ECG: ischemia, arrhythmia, LV hypertrophy. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- BNP or NT-proBNP when the diagnosis is uncertain. Also for prognosis. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Troponin: ACS, and prognosis (often modestly elevated). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Chest radiograph: pulmonary edema, effusions, other causes. Lung ultrasound (B-lines) is a sensitive adjunct. ^[esc-hf-2026]
- BMP, Mg²⁺, CBC, liver panel, TSH, glucose, iron studies (ferritin, transferrin saturation). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- Transthoracic echocardiogram if new heart failure or a change in clinical status. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

#### Natriuretic peptides
- **Acute heart failure unlikely:** ^[esc-hf-2026]
  - BNP <100 pg/mL
  - NT-proBNP <300 pg/mL
- **Lower in:** ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
  - Obesity (BNP and NT-proBNP)
  - Values may be low despite heart failure
- **Higher in:** ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
  - Age, AF, CKD, sepsis, PE (BNP and NT-proBNP)
  - Interpret with the clinical picture
- **Sacubitril-valsartan:** ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
  - BNP: ↑ (neprilysin substrate)
  - NT-proBNP: not affected
  - Use NT-proBNP on sacubitril-valsartan

#### Further testing
- New heart failure: evaluate for ischemia and other cardiomyopathies (as outpatient or once stable). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Right heart catheterization: unclear filling pressures, hypotension, worsening kidney function or no response to therapy. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

## Treatment
1. Respiratory support
   - Oxygen for SpO₂ <90%. Not routinely without hypoxemia. ^[esc-hf-2026]
   - Respiratory distress: noninvasive ventilation (CPAP or BiPAP) to improve gas exchange and reduce intubation. ^[esc-hf-2026]
   - Upright position. ^[common-practice]

2. IV loop diuretic
   - Start promptly when congested. IV, not oral, because of gut edema. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - Bolus or continuous infusion are equivalent. A higher dose gives more diuresis, with more transient creatinine rise. ^[dose-2011]

   {{dose:furosemide-iv-adhf}}

   - Check response early: spot urine Na⁺ at 2 h and urine output over 6 h (see Monitor). Urine Na⁺–guided diuresis in the first days may be considered (weak). ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019]
   - Inadequate response: double the loop dose. ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019]

3. Diuretic resistance
   - Persistent congestion despite an escalated loop dose: add a thiazide-type diuretic (metolazone, hydrochlorothiazide) or acetazolamide. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, hfa-diuretics-2019, advor-2022]
   - Monitor K⁺, Mg²⁺ and Na⁺ closely. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, hfa-diuretics-2019, advor-2022]

   {{dose:metolazone-adhf}}
   {{dose:acetazolamide-adhf}}

   - Refractory: consider ultrafiltration or kidney replacement therapy (limited evidence), or hemodynamic guidance. Cardiology. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - A modest creatinine rise during effective decongestion should not stop diuresis. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

4. Vasodilator
   - Consider IV nitroglycerin with diuretics if SBP >110 mm Hg (weak recommendation). First line in hypertensive pulmonary edema. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]

   {{dose:nitroglycerin-iv}}

   - Avoid with hypotension, severe aortic stenosis, hypertrophic obstructive cardiomyopathy, or phosphodiesterase-5 inhibitor use. ^[common-practice, label-ntg-premix-2016]

5. Hypoperfusion
   - Cold profile or cardiogenic shock: ICU, inotropes (dobutamine, milrinone) and vasopressors (norepinephrine) to maintain perfusion. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Mechanical support by the shock team. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Inotropes only for hypoperfusion, not routinely. No routine opioids. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - Treat the cause: urgent revascularization for ACS, cardioversion for unstable arrhythmia, surgery for acute valve lesions. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

6. GDMT in hospital
   - Continue existing GDMT unless hemodynamically unstable or contraindicated. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - A mild creatinine or blood pressure change is not a reason to stop. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Cardiogenic shock or hypoperfusion: hold β-blocker. Reduce or hold RAAS inhibitors with hypotension, hyperkalemia or AKI. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
   - HFrEF: start missing pillars once clinically stable: ARNI (or ACE inhibitor or ARB), evidence-based β-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, strong-hf-2022]
   - β-blocker: low dose, only once stable (near-euvolemic, diuretic dose stable, off IV vasodilators and inotropes). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, strong-hf-2022]
   - SGLT2 inhibitor can be started in hospital once stable, at any LVEF ^[empulse-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - Not with type 1 diabetes or ketoacidosis risk ^[empulse-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - Check the label {{calc:ckd-epi}} limit ^[empulse-2022, esc-hf-2026]
   - Doses and titration: Wave 2 heart failure cards. ^[common-practice]

7. Supportive care
   - VTE prophylaxis. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Daily weights, intake and output. Sodium restriction per local practice (evidence limited). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
   - Fluid restriction for hyponatremia or severe congestion. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Stop offending drugs (NSAIDs, nondihydropyridine calcium channel blockers in HFrEF, thiazolidinediones). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
   - Iron deficiency (ferritin <100 ng/mL, or 100–299 ng/mL with transferrin saturation <20%) with reduced LVEF: IV iron, before discharge. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]

## Monitor
- Response at 2 h: spot urine Na⁺ {{threshold:adhf-urine-na-response}} ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019]
- Over 6 h: urine output {{threshold:adhf-uop-response}} ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019]
- Below these, intensify. The 2026 cutoffs are the top of the urine Na⁺ range and the bottom of the urine output range. ^[esc-hf-2026, hfa-diuretics-2019]
- Daily: weight, intake and output, BMP and Mg²⁺ (K⁺ and Mg²⁺ fall with diuresis), congestion signs. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Goal: resolution of congestion (JVP, edema, orthopnea) before discharge. Residual congestion predicts readmission. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Hypotension, rising lactate, worsening mentation or oliguria: reassess perfusion. ^[esc-hf-2026]

## Escalation
- ICU: cardiogenic shock, need for inotropes or vasopressors, respiratory failure despite noninvasive ventilation. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Cardiology: new heart failure, ACS, significant valve disease, diuretic resistance, persistent worsening kidney function. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Advanced heart failure team: repeated admissions, inotrope dependence, intolerance of GDMT, end-organ dysfunction. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Palliative care for advanced disease. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: SGLT2 inhibitors, up-titration
:::compare SGLT2 inhibitor for LVEF >40%
- AHA/ACC/HFSA 2022: Reasonable (weaker recommendation) for LVEF >40%. Published before the DELIVER trial. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- ESC 2026: Foundational therapy at any LVEF (with an MRA in preserved LVEF). In-hospital start recommended after stabilization. Reduced LVEF now means <50%. ^[esc-hf-2026]
:::

:::compare After a heart failure admission
- AHA/ACC/HFSA 2022: Start GDMT in hospital once stable. Follow-up within 7 days. Published before STRONG-HF. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- ESC 2026: Rapid initiation and up-titration before discharge and frequent visits over the first 6 weeks (recommended, from STRONG-HF). ^[esc-hf-2026, strong-hf-2022]
:::

### Special Populations: HFpEF, kidney disease, right heart failure
- HFpEF: ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
  - Diuretics for congestion ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
  - Treat hypertension, AF and ischemia ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
  - SGLT2 inhibitor ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, esc-hf-2026]
- CKD: higher loop doses needed. MRA and SGLT2 inhibitor limits by {{calc:ckd-epi}}. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, hfa-diuretics-2019]
- Predominant right heart failure: avoid excess preload reduction and hypotension. ^[common-practice]
- Hypertrophic cardiomyopathy or severe aortic stenosis: avoid vasodilators and aggressive preload reduction. ^[common-practice]
- Pregnancy and peripartum: cardiology and obstetrics. Avoid ACE inhibitors, ARBs, ARNI, MRAs, SGLT2 inhibitors, ivabradine and vericiguat (fetal harm). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

### Complications: Kidney, electrolytes, arrhythmia
- Worsening kidney function (cardiorenal), hypokalemia, hypomagnesemia, hyponatremia, contraction alkalosis. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, hfa-diuretics-2019]
- Arrhythmias, especially with hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia. ^[common-practice]
- Cardiogenic shock. Readmission and death after discharge. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

### After the Acute Phase: Discharge, follow-up
- Discharge when euvolemic on an oral diuretic dose with a plan for adjustment. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Visit within 7 days and a phone call within 3 days. Labs (K⁺, creatinine) within 1–2 weeks after starting or titrating GDMT. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Education: daily weights, sodium intake, medication adherence, symptoms that should prompt a call. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Cardiac rehabilitation (HFrEF) ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Influenza and pneumococcal vaccination ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- ICD and CRT assessment after GDMT optimization ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]

### Evidence: Key trials
- DOSE (2011): bolus versus continuous and low versus high (2.5 times oral) furosemide did not differ in symptoms or creatinine at 72 h. ^[dose-2011]
- DOSE: high dose diuresed more, with more transient creatinine rise. ^[dose-2011]
- ADVOR (2022): acetazolamide added to loop diuretics improved successful decongestion at 3 days. ^[advor-2022]
- STRONG-HF (2022): rapid up-titration of GDMT with close follow-up after admission lowered 180-day death or heart failure readmission. ^[strong-hf-2022]

### Why the Treatment Order: Breathing, congestion, cause
- Hypoxemia and pulmonary edema kill first ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Decongestion relieves most symptoms ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- Perfusion problems need inotropes before diuretics work ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]
- GDMT then prevents the next admission ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022, common-practice]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology: Congestion
- Heart failure is a leading cause of hospitalization in adults over 65. Post-discharge readmission and mortality are high. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Most admissions follow gradual fluid accumulation from neurohormonal activation. Some are rapid fluid redistribution (hypertensive pulmonary edema). ^[esc-hf-2026]

## Pearls
- Weight loss alone is a poor marker: decongest to the clinical exam (JVP, orthopnea, edema). ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Creatinine rising while the patient decongests is usually hemodynamic, not injury: keep going if congestion persists. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
- Flash pulmonary edema with hypertension responds fastest to vasodilators. The patient may not be volume overloaded. ^[esc-hf-2026]
- Measure natriuretic peptide as NT-proBNP in patients on sacubitril-valsartan. ^[aha-acc-hfsa-hf-2022]
