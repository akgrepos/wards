## Do Now
1. Shock if torsades persists
   - If: torsades that does not stop on its own, ventricular fibrillation, or no pulse ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Check: pads on. Immediate unsynchronized shock. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, aha-acls-2025]
2. IV magnesium
   - If: torsades or ECG signs of impending torsades, whatever the serum magnesium ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

   {{dose:mgso4-iv-torsades}}

   - Warning: Hypotension ^[label-mgso4-2021]
3. Remove the triggers
   - If: torsades has stopped, or ECG signs of impending torsades are present ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Stop every QT-prolonging drug. Replace potassium. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Check: heart rate. Temporary pacing for bradycardia or long pauses. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Move to the highest level of ECG monitoring, where immediate defibrillation is available. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## At a Glance
- {{calc:qtc}} >500 ms, or a rise of 60 ms or more after an at-risk drug, calls for prompt action. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Torsades is a polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with a twisting pattern. It can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Stop the offending drug, replace potassium, give IV magnesium, and pace if there is bradycardia or a long pause. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Magnesium is first-line for torsades even when the serum magnesium is normal. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Torsades that does not stop on its own needs an immediate shock. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Check each QT-prolonging drug on a maintained list, since risk varies by drug. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, crediblemeds-list]

## Definition and Staging
- Acquired long QT comes from drugs, electrolyte disorders or slow rates, not a congenital channel defect. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Normal {{calc:qtc}} upper limits (99th percentile) are 470 ms in men and 480 ms in women. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- A {{calc:qtc}} >500 ms is highly abnormal in both sexes. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- The >500 ms risk sign does not apply to QT prolongation from amiodarone or verapamil. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Torsades de pointes is a polymorphic ventricular tachycardia with a pattern of twisting points. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- It is often short-lived and self-limited, but it can degenerate into ventricular fibrillation and sudden death. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## Severity
:::scale Corrected QT thresholds
- Normal: up to 470 ms in men and 480 ms in women. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Prolonged: above these limits. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Highly abnormal: >500 ms. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Prompt action: >500 ms, or a rise of 60 ms or more from the pre-drug baseline, especially with other ECG signs of impending torsades. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
:::

## Causes
- **Antiarrhythmics:** quinidine, procainamide, disopyramide, sotalol, dofetilide, ibutilide. Torsades incidence is 1% to 10%. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Amiodarone:** prolongs the QT markedly but rarely causes torsades. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Antipsychotics and opioids:** haloperidol, thioridazine, methadone. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Antidepressants:** tricyclics, and citalopram or trazodone in overdose. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Antibiotics:** erythromycin, clarithromycin, azithromycin and fluoroquinolones. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, aha-drug-arrhythmias-2020]
- **Antimalarials:** chloroquine and hydroxychloroquine, alone or with azithromycin. ^[aha-drug-arrhythmias-2020]
- **Antiemetics:** droperidol and ondansetron. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, crediblemeds-list]
- **Electrolytes:** hypokalemia and hypomagnesemia are established risk factors. Hypocalcemia rarely causes torsades. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Slow rates:** sinus bradycardia, complete AV block, or a long pause after a premature beat. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Higher drug levels:** overdose, CYP3A4 inhibitors (erythromycin, ketoconazole), liver or kidney disease. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Kidney clearance:** dofetilide and sotalol at ordinary doses in kidney failure raise torsades risk. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- **Drug risk level:** check each drug on a maintained QT drug list rather than from memory. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, crediblemeds-list]

## Presentation
- Palpitations, syncope or cardiac arrest, often first seen as a run on the monitor. ^[common-practice]
- A premature beat, a compensatory pause, then another premature beat (short-long-short) often starts a run. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Torsades after a drug exposure can be the first sign of congenital long QT. Ask about syncope and sudden death in the family. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## Diagnosis
- Measure the QT in the 12-lead lead with a T wave of at least 2 mm and a clear T-wave end. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- If the T-wave end is hard to find (biphasic, notched, U wave), draw a line from the T peak along the steepest downslope. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- A QT rise from a new bundle branch block is not acquired long QT. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- With a bundle branch block, subtract the extra QRS width, or measure the J-T interval from the end of the QRS. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Use the same adjustment method on every ECG for that patient. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Bazett overcorrects above about 85 beats/min. Fridericia is the common alternative. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- ECG signs of impending torsades after a drug starts: ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
  - A {{calc:qtc}} rise of 60 ms from baseline, or {{calc:qtc}} >500 ms.
  - T-U wave distortion that grows in the beat after a pause.
  - Visible T-wave alternans.
  - New ventricular ectopy, couplets or nonsustained polymorphic VT after a pause.
- Check potassium and magnesium. Check calcium too, since low calcium also prolongs the QT. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Risk factors: older age, female sex (about 2-fold), heart disease, kidney or liver dysfunction, slow rates, more than one QT-prolonging drug. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Before starting a QT-prolonging drug, weigh the benefit against the torsades risk. Prefer a non-QT-prolonging alternative of equal efficacy. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- The {{calc:tisdale}} predicts {{calc:qtc}} prolongation in hospitalized patients. ^[tisdale-qt-risk-2013]
  - Inputs: age, sex, loop diuretic, potassium, admission {{calc:qtc}}, acute MI, sepsis, heart failure, number of QT-prolonging drugs.

:::scale Hospital risk score bands
- Low (<7): QT prolongation in 15%. ^[tisdale-qt-risk-2013]
- Moderate (7–10): 37%. ^[tisdale-qt-risk-2013]
- High (≥11): 73%. ^[tisdale-qt-risk-2013]
:::

- Evidence is limited. The score came from one hospital's cardiac units. ^[tisdale-qt-risk-2013]
  - The outcome was {{calc:qtc}} ≥500 ms or a rise of 60 ms or more.

## Treatment
1. Stop the trigger
   - Stop the offending drug and other QT-prolonging drugs. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Where an alternative is equally effective, choose the one that does not prolong the QT. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
2. Give IV magnesium
   - First-line to end torsades, whatever the serum magnesium. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

   {{dose:mgso4-iv-torsades}}

   - Repeat the dose if torsades persists. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Keep magnesium normal afterward. For repletion see [[renal-electrolyte-repletion|Electrolyte Repletion]]. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
3. Replace potassium
   - Keep potassium and magnesium normal in patients at risk. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Targets: potassium >4 mEq/L and magnesium >2 mg/dL. ^[aha-drug-arrhythmias-2020]
   - A potassium of 4.5 to 5 mEq/L may be considered, but there is little evidence. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Dosing and cautions are in [[renal-hypokalemia|Hypokalemia]]. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
4. Prevent pauses
   - Temporary pacing at rates above 70 beats/min can prevent the pauses that trigger torsades. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Recurrent torsades with bradycardia despite magnesium: consider overdrive pacing or isoproterenol. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, aha-drug-arrhythmias-2020]
   - Isoproterenol is for acquired long QT only. Avoid it in congenital long QT or acute coronary syndrome. ^[esc-va-2022]
   - Use isoproterenol under cardiology guidance. ^[common-practice]
5. Shock if it persists
   - Immediate direct-current cardioversion for torsades that does not stop or turns into ventricular fibrillation. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## Monitor
- Document {{calc:qtc}} before, and at least every 8 to 12 hours after, starting or raising a QT-prolonging drug or an overdose. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Measure more often if the {{calc:qtc}} is prolonged. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Monitor the QT in these situations: ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
  - Starting a drug known to cause torsades.
  - Overdose of a drug that can cause arrhythmia.
  - New bradyarrhythmia.
  - Severe hypokalemia or hypomagnesemia.
- Continuous monitoring alarm: a rise >60 ms from baseline, or {{calc:qtc}} >500 ms on 3 consecutive measurements (about 15 minutes). ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- {{calc:qtc}} >500 ms or a rise of 60 ms or more: switch to an alternative drug, check interactions, correct potassium and magnesium. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Keep a defibrillator at hand and do not send the patient off the unit for tests. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Where benefit clearly outweighs risk, QT prolongation should not stop a needed drug. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## Escalation
- Signs of impending torsades: move to the highest level of ECG monitoring with immediate defibrillation. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Recurrent torsades despite magnesium, electrolytes and drug removal: call cardiology for pacing or isoproterenol. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, common-practice]
- Torsades without a clear drug or electrolyte cause, or with a family history of syncope or sudden death: suspect congenital long QT and call cardiology. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- For agitation and psychotropic choices in a patient with a long QT see [[psych-acute-agitation|Acute Agitation]] and [[psych-lithium-psychotropic-safety|Lithium and Psychotropic Safety]]. ^[common-practice]

## More Information
### Evidence: Why the thresholds are cautious
- The 500 ms and 60 ms limits come from a consensus statement, not a trial. Torsades is rare, so no trial tests a threshold. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, common-practice]
- Magnesium for torsades rests on case series and expert consensus. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]

## Pearls
- Risk stacks: torsades risk rises sharply with more than one QT-prolonging drug, or a drug plus a CYP3A4 inhibitor. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Women have about twice the torsades risk of men in hospital. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Amiodarone prolongs the QT but rarely causes torsades. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Intravenous dosing gives higher heart exposure than the same drug by mouth. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Bradycardia and a pause turn a borderline QT into torsades. Treat the slow rate. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Correct potassium and magnesium before and during any QT-prolonging drug. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
