## Do Now
1. IV fluids
   - If: DKA or HHS ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - Check: volume status; heart failure, dialysis or older age need smaller boluses and frequent reassessment. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:crystalloid-dka}}

2. Potassium before insulin
   - If: K⁺ ≤5.0 mEq/L, once urine output is established ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - Check: K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L: replace first and hold insulin. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:kcl-dka-maintenance}}

   - K⁺ >5.0 mEq/L: no potassium; recheck every 2 hours. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
3. Insulin infusion, DKA
   - If: DKA or mixed DKA/HHS, K⁺ >3.5 mEq/L ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:insulin-regular-dka}}

   - Warning: Hypokalemia, hypoglycemia ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
or. Insulin infusion, HHS
   - If: HHS without significant ketoacidosis, K⁺ >3.5 mEq/L ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:insulin-regular-hhs}}

   - Warning: Hypokalemia ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

5. Add dextrose, keep insulin
   - If: glucose <250 mg/dL, or euglycemic DKA from the start ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:dextrose-dka}}

   - DKA: lower the infusion to half its starting rate (the HHS rate); HHS: keep its rate. Never stop insulin for falling glucose. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
6. Find the precipitant
   - Infection, insulin omission, new diabetes, MI, stroke, pancreatitis, SGLT2 inhibitors, glucocorticoids, alcohol or drugs, pregnancy. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - Check: ECG, cultures, lipase and pregnancy test as indicated; stop SGLT2 inhibitors. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

:::note
- Bicarbonate only for pH <7.0. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Continue insulin until DKA resolves: β-hydroxybutyrate <0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃⁻ ≥18 mEq/L. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
:::

## At a Glance
- Order: fluids, then potassium, then insulin; K⁺ must be >3.5 mEq/L before insulin. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- DKA: glucose ≥200 mg/dL or known diabetes, β-hydroxybutyrate ≥3.0 mmol/L, and pH <7.3 or HCO₃⁻ <18 mEq/L; about 1 in 10 is euglycemic. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- HHS: glucose ≥600 mg/dL and effective osmolality >300 mOsm/kg without significant ketoacidosis; correct slowly. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Glucose <250 mg/dL: add dextrose and lower DKA insulin to half its starting rate; never stop insulin to treat falling glucose. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Resolution: β-hydroxybutyrate <0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃⁻ ≥18 mEq/L; overlap SC basal insulin before stopping the infusion. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Always look for the precipitant, especially infection and insulin omission. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

## Severity
{{criteria:dka-hhs}}

#### DKA severity
{{criteria:dka-severity}}

- ICU: severe DKA, HHS, altered mental status, or a critical-illness precipitant (MI, GI bleeding, sepsis). ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Mild or moderate uncomplicated DKA can be treated in the ED or a step-down unit with close nursing. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- UK markers of severity prompting high-dependency care include β-hydroxybutyrate >6.0 mmol/L, HCO₃⁻ <5.0 mEq/L, pH <7.0, K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L on arrival, {{calc:gcs}} <12, SBP <90 mm Hg; in HHS, osmolality >350 mOsm/kg or Na⁺ >160 mEq/L. ^[jbds-dka-2023, jbds-hhs-2022]

## Causes
- **Infection:** [!most-common] pneumonia, UTI; the most common precipitant worldwide. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **Insufficient insulin:** missed or rationed insulin, pump failure, new-onset diabetes (type 1 or ketosis-prone type 2). ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **Acute illness:** [!cannot-miss] MI, stroke, pancreatitis, PE, trauma. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **Drugs:** SGLT2 inhibitors (often euglycemic), glucocorticoids, antipsychotics, sympathomimetics, immune checkpoint inhibitors. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **Substances and other:** alcohol, cocaine and other drugs; pregnancy. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **HHS in particular:** older adults with infection or limited access to water (impaired thirst, dependence on others). ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- **Mimics of ketoacidosis:** alcoholic ketoacidosis, starvation ketosis, lactic acidosis, toxic alcohols, salicylates, uremia. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, common-practice]

## Presentation
- Both: polyuria, polydipsia, weight loss, volume depletion. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- DKA: develops over hours to days; nausea, vomiting and abdominal pain (over half); Kussmaul respirations, fruity breath; usually alert. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- HHS: develops over days to a week; profound dehydration; confusion, lethargy, focal deficits or seizures. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, common-practice]
- Abdominal pain that persists after correcting dehydration and acidosis needs its own workup. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

## Diagnosis
#### Initial tests
- Glucose, BMP with {{calc:anion-gap}}, venous blood gas, β-hydroxybutyrate, CBC, phosphate, magnesium, {{calc:serum-osmolality}}. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- ECG (hyperkalemia, ischemia); cultures, chest radiograph, lipase, troponin, HbA1c and pregnancy test as indicated. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, common-practice]

#### Interpretation
- Measure β-hydroxybutyrate directly: urine nitroprusside tests detect acetoacetate, so they underestimate ketosis early and overestimate it during recovery. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- {{calc:anion-gap|Anion gap}} (>12 mEq/L is high) helps in mixed acid–base disorders or without ketone testing, but not to define resolution. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Sodium rises about 1.6 mEq/L for every 100 mg/dL fall in glucose; use measured Na⁺ for the {{calc:anion-gap}}. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, common-practice]
- K⁺ is often normal or high on arrival despite a total-body deficit. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Venous pH is adequate; arterial gas only if oxygenation is in question. ^[common-practice]
- Lipase can be elevated without pancreatitis. ^[common-practice]

## Treatment
1. Fluids
   {{dose:crystalloid-dka}}

   - Balanced crystalloid or 0.9% saline; balanced solutions linked to faster resolution and less hyperchloremic acidosis. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - HHS: the expected rise in Na⁺ as glucose falls is not a reason to switch to 0.45% saline. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

2. Potassium
   - Replace before and with insulin; target K⁺ 4–5 mEq/L. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:kcl-dka-maintenance}}

   - Magnesium: replace if low (dosing: [[renal-electrolyte-repletion|electrolyte repletion]]). ^[common-practice]

3. Insulin
   - DKA: IV regular insulin by continuous infusion once K⁺ >3.5 mEq/L; bolus only if starting the infusion will be delayed. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:insulin-regular-dka}}
   {{dose:insulin-regular-dka-bolus}}

   - Mild DKA (the consensus text also allows uncomplicated moderate DKA): SC rapid-acting insulin every 1–2 hours is an alternative. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, ada-soc-hospital-2026]

   {{dose:insulin-rapid-sc-dka}}

   - HHS: lower fixed rate; mixed DKA/HHS uses the DKA rate. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:insulin-regular-hhs}}

   - Continue the home basal insulin, or add low-dose basal insulin early, to prevent rebound. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, ada-soc-hospital-2026]

   {{dose:insulin-glargine-dka}}

4. Dextrose
   - Glucose targets until resolution: DKA 150–200 mg/dL; HHS 200–250 mg/dL. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:dextrose-dka}}

5. Bicarbonate and phosphate
   - Bicarbonate only for pH <7.0. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:sodium-bicarbonate-dka}}

   - Phosphate only with severe hypophosphatemia and weakness or respiratory or cardiac compromise. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:kphos-dka}}

6. HHS: correct slowly
   - Glucose fall ≤90–120 mg/dL per hour, osmolality fall 3–8 mOsm/kg per hour, Na⁺ fall ≤10 mEq/L in 24 hours. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - Usual time to correct hyperglycemia: 8–10 hours. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

7. Resolution and transition to SC insulin
   - DKA resolved: β-hydroxybutyrate <0.6 mmol/L and pH ≥7.3 or HCO₃⁻ ≥18 mEq/L; do not use the {{calc:anion-gap}} or urine ketones. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - HHS resolved: osmolality <300 mOsm/kg, glucose <250 mg/dL, urine output >0.5 mL/kg/h, cognition improved. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
   - Give SC basal insulin at least 1–2 hours before stopping the infusion (ADA 2026: 2–4 hours). Switch once able to eat; if not eating, basal plus correction insulin every 4–6 hours. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, ada-soc-hospital-2026]

   {{dose:insulin-transition-dka}}

8. Prevent complications
   - VTE prophylaxis unless thrombosis is suspected (prothrombotic, especially HHS). ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

   {{dose:enoxaparin-prophylaxis}}

   - Stop SGLT2 inhibitors; do not restart in the hospital. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

- Every 2–4 hours until stable: electrolytes, phosphate, creatinine, β-hydroxybutyrate, venous pH; osmolality in HHS. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Fluid balance, mental status and urine output; creatinine daily. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Hyperchloremic non-gap acidosis during recovery is expected and self-limited; it is not persistent DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- New headache, confusion or declining consciousness: suspect cerebral edema. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

## Escalation
- ICU: severe DKA, HHS, altered mental status, hemodynamic instability or a critical precipitant. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Endocrinology or the inpatient diabetes team: new diagnosis, recurrent DKA, insulin pump users, pregnancy, transition planning. ^[jbds-dka-2023, common-practice]
- Obstetrics at once for DKA in pregnancy. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: US vs UK
:::compare Diagnosis and fluids
- US-led consensus 2024: HCO₃⁻ <18 mEq/L; 0.9% saline or balanced crystalloid. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- UK 2023: HCO₃⁻ <15 mEq/L; 0.9% saline by default on the ward. ^[jbds-dka-2023]
:::

:::compare Insulin and potassium
- US-led consensus 2024: lower insulin to half the starting rate at glucose <250 mg/dL with dextrose 5–10%; potassium added to fluids at K⁺ 3.5–5.0 mEq/L. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- UK 2023: consider halving at glucose <14 mmol/L, with 10% glucose; potassium added at K⁺ 3.5–5.5 mEq/L, at a higher fixed concentration per liter. ^[jbds-dka-2023]
:::

:::compare HHS insulin
- US-led consensus 2024: fixed-rate insulin from the start (lower rate without ketoacidosis). ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- UK 2022: insulin from the start only with ketonemia (β-hydroxybutyrate >1.0 mmol/L); otherwise start once fluids are adequate and glucose stops falling. ^[jbds-hhs-2022]
:::

### Special Populations: SGLT2 inhibitors, pregnancy, kidney failure
- SGLT2 inhibitors: often euglycemic; start dextrose with insulin; stop the drug and do not routinely restart it in type 2 diabetes after DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- SGLT2 inhibitors are not recommended in type 1 diabetes. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Before scheduled surgery, stop SGLT2 inhibitors 3 days ahead (ertugliflozin 4 days). ^[ada-soc-hospital-2026]
- Pregnancy: DKA can occur at lower glucose; immediate obstetric and medical care. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Dialysis or ESKD: careful fluids and potassium; more hyperkalemia and hyponatremia, lower ketones. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Older or frail adults: more HHS and mixed crises; smaller fluid boluses. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Immune checkpoint inhibitors: autoimmune diabetes often presents as DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

### Complications: Hypoglycemia, hypokalemia, edema
- Hypoglycemia in 16–28% of patients; reduce it with glucose checks every 1–2 hours, lowering insulin and adding dextrose at glucose <250 mg/dL. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Hypokalemia; severe hypokalemia raises mortality. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Cerebral edema: rare in adults but often fatal; mannitol or hypertonic saline without delaying for imaging. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, jbds-dka-2023]
- Osmotic demyelination in HHS with rapid sodium shifts. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Arterial and venous thrombosis, AKI (about half), rhabdomyolysis. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, jbds-dka-2023]

### After the Acute Phase: Prevent recurrence
- Readmission is common, half within 2 weeks; follow-up within 2–4 weeks. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Education: insulin technique, glucose and blood ketone monitoring, sick-day rules (never stop basal insulin), when to seek care. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, ada-soc-glycemic-2026]
- Offer CGM at discharge; ensure insulin and supplies are affordable and in hand. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Screen for depression and social needs; psychological support for recurrent DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- New ketosis-prone diabetes: antibody and C-peptide testing to guide long-term insulin. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024, common-practice]

### Why the Treatment Order: Fluids, potassium, insulin
- Fluids restore perfusion and lower glucose on their own; insulin drives potassium into cells, so starting it with K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L risks arrhythmia. Insulin, not dextrose withdrawal, clears ketones. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology: Insulin deficiency
- DKA: absolute insulin deficiency and counterregulatory hormones drive lipolysis and ketogenesis. HHS: residual insulin prevents ketosis while osmotic diuresis causes severe dehydration. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- About a third of crises are mixed DKA/HHS; HHS mortality is 5–10 times that of DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

## Pearls
- A normal or high K⁺ on arrival hides a large total-body deficit. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Euglycemic DKA is easy to miss: check ketones in any unwell patient on an SGLT2 inhibitor with an {{calc:anion-gap}}. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- The {{calc:anion-gap}} closing late because of hyperchloremia is not persistent DKA: follow β-hydroxybutyrate and pH. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
- Stopping the infusion without SC basal insulin overlap causes rebound DKA. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]
