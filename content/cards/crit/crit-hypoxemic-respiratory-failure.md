## Do Now
1. Airway and oxygen
   - If: SpO₂ below target or respiratory distress ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
   - Check: airway patency, mental status, code status. ^[common-practice]
   - Start oxygen: nonrebreather mask if severely hypoxemic, then titrate. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
   - Target SpO₂ 94–98% in most; 88–92% with hypercapnia risk. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
2. Rapid cause search
   - Blood gas, chest radiograph, ECG; bedside ultrasound of lungs, heart and leg veins. ^[common-practice]
   - Treat what is fast to reverse: tension pneumothorax, flash pulmonary edema, opioid overdose, bronchospasm, mucus plug, PE. ^[common-practice]
3. High-flow nasal oxygen
   - If: hypoxemia or distress despite conventional oxygen ^[ats-nirs-2026, ssc-sepsis-2026]
   - Start about 50 L/min with FiO₂ 1.0, then titrate FiO₂ to the target. ^[florali-2015]
   - ROX index at 2, 6 and 12 hours. ^[roca-rox-2019]
4. NIV or CPAP instead
   - If: hypercapnia, cardiogenic pulmonary edema, or high-flow not tolerated ^[ats-nirs-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017]
   - Check: also none of recent upper airway or upper GI surgery, upper GI bleeding, hemodynamic instability. ^[common-practice]
   - Check: none of: arrest, unable to protect airway or clear secretions, vomiting, facial trauma, uncontrolled agitation. ^[common-practice]
5. Intubate early if failing
   - If: worsening gas exchange, persistent tachypnea or effort, low ROX, altered mentation, shock ^[ats-nirs-2026, roca-rox-2019]
   - Preoxygenate with high-flow or NIV; call ICU. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
6. Lung-protective ventilation
   - ARDS: tidal volume {{threshold:ards-tidal-volume}}, plateau pressure ≤30 cm H₂O. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, ats-esicm-sccm-ards-2017]
   - No ARDS (sepsis): tidal volume {{threshold:tidal-volume-no-ards}}. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026]

:::note
- Noninvasive support must not delay an indicated intubation. ^[ats-nirs-2026, common-practice]
:::

## At a Glance
- Give oxygen first, then find the cause: blood gas, chest radiograph, ECG, ultrasound. ^[common-practice]
- Target SpO₂ 94–98% in most patients; 88–92% when at risk of hypercapnia. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- High-flow nasal oxygen is the first noninvasive step: fewer intubations than conventional oxygen. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- NIV for hypercapnia; CPAP or NIV for cardiogenic pulmonary edema. ^[ats-nirs-2026, ers-ats-niv-2017]
- Track response hourly (ROX index, work of breathing); intubate early when noninvasive support fails. ^[ats-nirs-2026, roca-rox-2019]
- Intubated with ARDS: low tidal volume, plateau ≤30 cm H₂O; prone positioning for moderate–severe ARDS. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, ats-esicm-sccm-ards-2017]

## Definition and Staging
- Hypoxemic (type 1): PaO₂ <60 mm Hg with normal or low PaCO₂; hypercapnic (type 2): PaCO₂ >45 mm Hg, acute when pH <7.35; both can coexist. ^[common-practice]
- PaO₂/FiO₂ (or SpO₂/FiO₂ when SpO₂ ≤97%) tracks severity; formal grades apply to ARDS (below). ^[ards-global-definition-2024]

{{criteria:ards-global-2024}}

- A–a gradient = [FiO₂ × (760 − 47) − PaCO₂/0.8] − PaO₂; normal about age/4 + 4 mm Hg on room air. ^[common-practice]

## First Steps
- Red flags for immediate help (rapid response, ICU, anesthesia): SpO₂ <80% or PaO₂ <55 mm Hg despite oxygen, RR >35, rising PaCO₂, exhaustion, altered mentation, unable to protect airway, shock. ^[common-practice]
- Look for an immediately reversible cause before anything else (tension pneumothorax, airway obstruction, opioid overdose). ^[common-practice]
- Confirm code status and goals early. ^[common-practice]

## Causes
- **V/Q mismatch:** [!most-common] pneumonia, pulmonary edema, COPD or asthma, PE, atelectasis, mucus plugging; improves with oxygen. ^[common-practice]
- **Shunt:** ARDS, lobar collapse, extensive pneumonia or edema, intracardiac or pulmonary vascular shunts; little response to 100% oxygen. ^[common-practice]
- **Hypoventilation:** opioids or sedatives, neuromuscular weakness, obesity hypoventilation; normal A–a gradient, high PaCO₂. ^[common-practice]
- **Diffusion limitation:** interstitial lung disease, pulmonary hypertension; worse with exertion. ^[common-practice]
- **Low inspired oxygen:** altitude; normal A–a gradient. ^[common-practice]
- **Cannot miss:** [!cannot-miss] tension pneumothorax, PE, flash pulmonary edema or ACS, ARDS, aspiration, upper airway obstruction, hemothorax or massive effusion. ^[common-practice]

## Presentation
#### Focused history
- Onset (sudden: PE, pneumothorax, aspiration, flash edema; over hours to days: pneumonia, ARDS), fever, sputum, chest pain, orthopnea, leg swelling. ^[common-practice]
- Opioids, sedatives, transfusion, fluids given, recent surgery or immobility, known COPD or heart failure. ^[common-practice]

#### Focused examination
- Work of breathing: RR, accessory muscles, paradoxical breathing, inability to speak in sentences. ^[common-practice]
- Mental status, cyanosis, JVP, crackles, wheeze, absent breath sounds with tracheal shift, unilateral leg swelling. ^[common-practice]
- Pulse oximetry overestimates saturation in darker skin; confirm with a blood gas. ^[sjoding-oximetry-2020, ssc-sepsis-2026]

## Diagnosis
#### First-line tests
- Arterial blood gas: PaO₂, PaCO₂, pH; calculate PaO₂/FiO₂ and the A–a gradient. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, common-practice]
- Chest radiograph; ECG; troponin and natriuretic peptide; CBC, BMP, lactate. ^[common-practice]
- Point-of-care ultrasound: B-lines (edema, ARDS), consolidation, effusion, absent lung sliding (pneumothorax), LV and RV function, DVT. ^[common-practice]

#### Interpretation
| Finding | Suggests |
|---|---|
| Normal A–a gradient, high PaCO₂ | Hypoventilation ^[common-practice] |
| High A–a gradient, corrects with oxygen | V/Q mismatch ^[common-practice] |
| High A–a gradient, little response to 100% oxygen | Shunt ^[common-practice] |
| Clear chest radiograph | PE, asthma or COPD, intracardiac shunt, early pneumonia ^[common-practice] |

#### Second line, by scenario
- Suspected PE: [[pulm-vte-diagnosis|VTE diagnosis]] workup, CT pulmonary angiography. ^[aha-acc-pe-2026]
- Bilateral infiltrates: echocardiogram to separate cardiogenic edema from ARDS. ^[ards-global-definition-2024, common-practice]
- Pneumonia: cultures and testing as for [[id-cap|community-acquired pneumonia]]. ^[common-practice]

## Treatment
#### Common initial orders
- Continuous pulse oximetry; oxygen titrated to target; head of bed raised. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, common-practice]
- First-line tests in Diagnosis. ^[common-practice]

#### Conventional oxygen
- Nasal cannula, then face mask or nonrebreather for higher needs; Venturi mask for a precise FiO₂ when hypercapnia is a risk. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- Avoid hyperoxia: titrate down once stable; a conservative target (88–92%) did not change mortality in ventilated adults. ^[uk-rox-2025, ssc-sepsis-2026]

#### High-flow nasal oxygen
- Recommended over conventional oxygen for acute hypoxemic respiratory failure; lowers intubation. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Suggested over conventional oxygen (PaO₂/FiO₂ <200 or SpO₂/FiO₂ <235) and over NIV as initial support in sepsis-related hypoxemia. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026]
- Start about 50 L/min with FiO₂ 1.0; titrate FiO₂ to the SpO₂ target, keep flow high while work of breathing is high. ^[florali-2015]
- Trial of awake prone positioning, as tolerated (evidence mostly from COVID-19). ^[ssc-sepsis-2026]

#### NIV and CPAP
- NIV or CPAP (facemask or helmet) is a suggested alternative to conventional oxygen; no recommendation between high-flow and NIV. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- NIV is strongly recommended for hypercapnic failure ([[pulm-copd-exacerbation|COPD exacerbation]]); high-flow only with mild acidemia (pH >7.25) and NIV promptly available. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- CPAP or NIV for cardiogenic pulmonary edema. ^[ers-ats-niv-2017]
- Hypoxemia or cardiogenic edema: CPAP or EPAP 5–10 cm H₂O, titrated to oxygenation and work of breathing. ^[common-practice]
- Hypercapnia: EPAP 3–5 cm H₂O; inspiratory pressure about 15, raised to 20–30 cm H₂O within 10–30 minutes; blood gas within 1 hour. ^[bts-ics-hypercapnic-2016]

#### Intubation
- Watch for nonimproving or worsening gas exchange, persistent tachypnea, signs of muscle fatigue; have intubation criteria agreed in advance. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Preoxygenate with high-flow or NIV rather than conventional oxygen. ^[ats-nirs-2026]

#### Initial ventilator settings
- Volume control. ARDS: tidal volume {{threshold:ards-tidal-volume}}; sepsis without ARDS: {{threshold:tidal-volume-no-ards}}. ^[ats-esicm-sccm-ards-2017, ssc-sepsis-2026]
- Plateau pressure ≤30 cm H₂O. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, ats-esicm-sccm-ards-2017]
- Start PEEP about 5 cm H₂O and FiO₂ 1.0; set the rate to match pre-intubation minute ventilation; in ARDS, titrate to SpO₂ 88–95% with a PEEP/FiO₂ table. ^[common-practice]
- Moderate–severe ARDS: higher PEEP, no prolonged recruitment maneuvers ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, ats-ards-2024]; prone positioning more than 12 hours a day. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026, ats-esicm-sccm-ards-2017]
- PaO₂/FiO₂ <150 with hypoxemia or ventilator targets not met despite sedation: consider neuromuscular blockade. ^[sccm-nmba-ards-2026]
- ARDS: corticosteroids suggested; VV-ECMO in selected severe cases. ^[ats-ards-2024, sccm-corticosteroids-2024]

#### Treat the cause
- Pneumonia: [[id-cap|antibiotics]]; heart failure: [[cardio-adhf|diuresis and vasodilators]]; COPD: [[pulm-copd-exacerbation|bronchodilators, steroids, NIV]]; sepsis: [[crit-sepsis|sepsis bundle]]. ^[common-practice]

## Monitor
:::times ROX index on high-flow, hours ^[roca-rox-2019]
- 2
- 6
- 12
:::

- ROX = (SpO₂/FiO₂)/RR (validated in pneumonia). ≥4.88 at 2, 6 or 12 hours predicts success; <2.85, <3.47 and <3.85 at 2, 6 and 12 hours predict failure. ^[roca-rox-2019]
- Continuous pulse oximetry, RR, work of breathing and mental status; a monitored bed for high-flow at high flow or FiO₂, and for NIV. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- Blood gas after starting or changing support. ^[bts-oxygen-2017, bts-ics-hypercapnic-2016]

## Escalation
- ICU: high-flow with high FiO₂ or rising needs, NIV in hypoxemic failure, low or falling ROX, intubation. ^[ats-nirs-2026, roca-rox-2019]
- Anesthesia or airway team: predicted difficult airway, upper airway obstruction. ^[common-practice]
- Interventional pulmonology or thoracic surgery: massive effusion, persistent pneumothorax, central airway obstruction. ^[common-practice]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: Oxygen target, high-flow vs NIV
:::compare SpO₂ target
- BTS 2017: 94–98% for most acutely ill adults; 88–92% with hypercapnia risk. ^[bts-oxygen-2017]
- SSC 2026: either a liberal or a conservative target, individualized. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026]
:::

:::compare High-flow vs NIV as first step
- ATS 2026: no recommendation between them; choose by patient, expertise and resources. ^[ats-nirs-2026]
- SSC 2026 (sepsis): high-flow suggested over NIV. ^[ssc-sepsis-2026]
:::

### Special Populations: Do-not-intubate, immunocompromise
- Do-not-intubate: NIV or high-flow as the ceiling of care, matched to goals. ^[common-practice]
- Immunocompromised patients: noninvasive support as for others; evaluate early for opportunistic infection. ^[common-practice]

### Complications: Of support
- NIV: aspiration, skin injury, delayed intubation with higher mortality. ^[ats-nirs-2026, common-practice]
- Invasive ventilation: ventilator-induced lung injury, ventilator-associated pneumonia, hemodynamic compromise. ^[common-practice]

### Evidence: Key trials
- FLORALI (2015): high-flow oxygen did not lower intubation overall versus standard oxygen or NIV; 90-day mortality was lower (secondary outcome) and intubation lower with PaO₂/FiO₂ ≤200. Excluded hypercapnia and cardiogenic edema. ^[florali-2015]
- UK-ROX (2025): a conservative SpO₂ target of 90% (88–92%) did not change 90-day mortality in ventilated ICU adults. ^[uk-rox-2025]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology: Five mechanisms
- Low inspired oxygen, hypoventilation, V/Q mismatch, shunt and diffusion limitation; only shunt resists supplemental oxygen. ^[common-practice]

## Pearls
- A normal SpO₂ does not exclude respiratory failure in a patient on oxygen; look at the work of breathing and PaCO₂. ^[common-practice]
- Clear chest radiograph with hypoxemia: think PE. ^[common-practice]
- High-flow buys time, not a diagnosis; reassess within 1–2 hours. ^[roca-rox-2019, ats-nirs-2026]
- Delayed intubation after failed noninvasive support is associated with worse outcomes. ^[ats-nirs-2026, common-practice]
