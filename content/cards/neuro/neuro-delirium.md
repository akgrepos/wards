## At a Glance
- Delirium is acute and fluctuating, with inattention at its core. It signals a medical cause, so search for and treat the cause. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]
- Screen high-risk patients with the {{calc:4at}} on the ward and the {{calc:cam-icu}} in the ICU. Hypoactive delirium is the most commonly missed. ^[nice-delirium-2023, padis-2018]
- First line is a multicomponent nonpharmacologic bundle plus stopping deliriogenic drugs (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids). ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023, ags-beers-2023]
- Antipsychotics only for severe distress or danger after de-escalation fails: lowest dose, shortest time, check the {{calc:qtc}}. Not used to prevent or shorten delirium. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]
- Avoid benzodiazepines unless the cause is alcohol or sedative withdrawal (or seizure, catatonia). ^[apa-delirium-2025, esaic-pod-2024]
- In ventilated patients prefer dexmedetomidine to benzodiazepines for sedation. ^[padis-2018, padis-2025, apa-delirium-2025]

## Severity
{{criteria:cam}}

{{criteria:4at}}

- Delirium is a medical emergency in that it predicts death, longer stay and long-term cognitive decline, including in its hypoactive form. ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
- Level of care: ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
  - ICU for ventilated or hemodynamically unstable patients ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
  - One-to-one observation or a sitter for wandering or danger to self ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
  - Restraints only as a last resort ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
- Rate agitation and sedation with the {{calc:rass}} in the ICU (target light sedation, most studies defined it as {{calc:rass|RASS}} −2 to +1). ^[padis-2018]

## Causes
- **Medications:** [!most-common] anticholinergics (diphenhydramine, hydroxyzine, oxybutynin, tricyclic antidepressants), benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, opioids (including meperidine), corticosteroids, H2 blockers, antiepileptics, dopamine agonists. New starts, dose changes and withdrawal. ^[ags-beers-2023, apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
- **Infection:** [!cannot-miss] urinary ([[id-complicated-uti|complicated UTI]]), pneumonia, bloodstream infection ([[crit-sepsis|sepsis]]), skin, meningitis or encephalitis, C. difficile. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]
- **Hypoxemia and hypercapnia:** [[crit-hypoxemic-respiratory-failure|hypoxemic respiratory failure]], COPD exacerbation, heart failure. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]
- **Metabolic:** [[renal-hyponatremia|hyponatremia]], hypernatremia, hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, hypercalcemia, uremia, hepatic encephalopathy, thyroid disease, hypothermia. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]
- **Withdrawal and intoxication:** [!cannot-miss] alcohol and benzodiazepine withdrawal ([[neuro-alcohol-withdrawal|alcohol withdrawal delirium]]), opioid, sedative or stimulant effects. ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
- **Neurologic:** [!cannot-miss] stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, seizure or postictal state, nonconvulsive status epilepticus, central nervous system infection, Wernicke encephalopathy. ^[common-practice]
- **Physiologic and environmental:** urinary retention, fecal impaction or constipation, uncontrolled pain, dehydration, sleep deprivation, immobility, lines and restraints, sensory loss (missing glasses or hearing aids), ICU environment. ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]
- **Postoperative and other:** anesthesia, major surgery, hip fracture, cardiac surgery. ^[esaic-pod-2024]
- **Predisposing factors:** age ≥65 years, dementia or cognitive impairment, severe illness, prior delirium, sensory impairment, frailty, polypharmacy. ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]

## Presentation
- Acute onset (hours to days) with fluctuation, worse at night. Inattention, disorganized thinking, altered level of consciousness, disorientation, perceptual disturbance. ^[apa-delirium-2025, inouye-cam-1990]
- Hyperactive: agitation, restlessness, hallucinations, pulling at lines. Easily recognized. ^[common-practice]
- Hypoactive: drowsy, withdrawn, slowed. Frequently missed or mistaken for depression, fatigue or dementia. ^[common-practice]
- Mixed: alternates between the two. ^[common-practice]
- Ask family or staff for the baseline: a change from baseline is the key. ^[nice-delirium-2023]
- Dementia raises the risk and can mask delirium: new inattention, a change in alertness or a rapid change from baseline suggests superimposed delirium. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]

## Diagnosis
#### Screening and assessment
- Screen at admission, daily and with any acute change in patients at risk. Ward: {{calc:4at}}. ICU or recovery: {{calc:cam-icu}} or ICDSC. ^[nice-delirium-2023, padis-2018]
- Confirm with a clinician's assessment of the {{calc:cam-icu}} or CAM features and a review of DSM-5-TR criteria. ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]
- Sedation level first: assess the {{calc:rass}}. At {{calc:rass|RASS}} −4 or −5 the {{calc:cam-icu}} cannot be done, so reassess later. ^[ely-camicu-2001, padis-2018]

#### Workup of reversible causes
- History, collateral and medication review (including recent starts, doses and withdrawal), vital signs with oxygen saturation, glucose, and a full physical examination with a neurologic examination. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]
- Initial tests: BMP (sodium, calcium, creatinine), glucose, CBC, urinalysis with culture when symptoms or signs suggest infection, ECG, oxygen saturation or blood gas. ^[common-practice]
- Add by clinical clues: blood cultures, chest radiograph, liver panel and ammonia, thyroid studies, magnesium, phosphate, B12, drug levels, toxicology. ^[common-practice]
- Bladder scan for retention and rectal examination for impaction. ^[common-practice]
- Neuroimaging, lumbar puncture or EEG only for focal findings, fever with meningismus, seizure concern, head trauma or no cause found. ^[common-practice]

#### Avoid
- Screening only by orientation or by the Mini-Mental State Examination. They miss delirium, which needs attention testing. ^[common-practice]
- Blaming dementia or "sundowning" before looking for a cause. ^[common-practice]

## Treatment
1. Treat causes
   - Find and treat the precipitant: infection, hypoxemia, metabolic derangement, pain, retention, constipation, withdrawal. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]
   - Medication review: stop or reduce anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, opioids, H2 blockers and corticosteroids where possible. Substitute safer drugs. ^[ags-beers-2023, apa-delirium-2025]
   - Treat pain, with acetaminophen and regional or opioid-sparing options before systemic opioids. ^[ags-beers-2023, padis-2018]
   - Alcohol use: consider withdrawal in daily drinkers, and give thiamine before glucose when Wernicke encephalopathy is possible (see [[neuro-alcohol-withdrawal|alcohol withdrawal]]). ^[common-practice]

2. Nonpharmacologic bundle (first line for everyone)
   - Reorientation, familiar objects, clock and calendar, family presence, consistent staff. ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]
   - Glasses, hearing aids and dentures in place. Adequate lighting. ^[nice-delirium-2023]
   - Sleep protection: lights and noise down at night, cluster care, no night-time vitals if stable, daylight and activity by day. ^[nice-delirium-2023, padis-2018]
   - Early mobilization and avoiding immobility. Avoid tethers (remove catheters, lines and telemetry as soon as possible). ^[nice-delirium-2023, padis-2018]
   - Hydration, nutrition, bowel and bladder care, oxygen when hypoxemic. ^[nice-delirium-2023]
   - Avoid physical restraints. Use a sitter or family. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
   - Structured multicomponent programs (HELP-type bundles) lower delirium incidence in older adults (OR 0.47). ^[help-meta-2018]

3. De-escalation
   - Calm, brief, simple communication. Reassure, do not argue with delusions. Address needs (pain, toileting, thirst). ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]
   - Attempt de-escalation before any drug. ^[nice-delirium-2023, apa-delirium-2025]

4. Antipsychotic for severe agitation (last resort)
   - Only for severe distress or danger to the patient or others, after de-escalation and cause treatment. Not to prevent delirium or shorten it. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]
   - Lowest dose, shortest time (usually 1 week or less), with a short-term order that forces reassessment. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]
   - Before the first dose: baseline {{calc:qtc}}, potassium, magnesium and QT-prolonging drugs reviewed. A {{calc:qtc|QTc}} >500 ms carries a 2- to 3-fold torsades risk. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, label-haloperidol-inj-2026]
   - After a dose, a {{calc:qtc|QTc}} >500 ms or a rise of ≥60 ms from baseline calls for prompt action: stop or switch the drug, correct electrolytes, review interacting drugs. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
   - Haloperidol injection is not approved for IV use. If given IV, monitor the ECG. ^[label-haloperidol-inj-2026]

   {{dose:haloperidol-delirium-low}}

   - ICU with ECG monitoring (the dose studied in AID-ICU, the 2025 PADIS update makes no recommendation for or against antipsychotics). ^[aid-icu-2022, padis-2025]

   {{dose:haloperidol-delirium-icu}}

   - Haloperidol is contraindicated in Parkinson disease and dementia with Lewy bodies. ^[label-haloperidol-inj-2026, nice-delirium-2023]
   - Atypical antipsychotics carry the same dementia boxed warning, and low-dose haloperidol and atypicals have similar efficacy and side-effect rates. ^[label-zyprexa-2026, esaic-pod-2024]
   - Olanzapine IM should not be combined with a parenteral benzodiazepine. ^[label-zyprexa-2026]
   - Stop the antipsychotic once symptoms resolve and do not continue it at discharge. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]

5. Sedation in the ventilated patient
   - Prefer dexmedetomidine to benzodiazepines for sedation, and the 2025 PADIS update suggests it over propofol (conditional). ^[padis-2018, padis-2025, apa-delirium-2025]
   - Dexmedetomidine for delirium-related agitation that prevents weaning or extubation. ^[padis-2018]

   {{dose:dexmedetomidine-icu-delirium}}

6. Benzodiazepines
   - Avoid for delirium. Use for alcohol or sedative withdrawal, catatonia, neuroleptic malignant syndrome, serotonin syndrome and seizure. ^[apa-delirium-2025, esaic-pod-2024]
   - Melatonin and ramelteon: the APA guideline advises against them for prevention or treatment. The 2025 PADIS update conditionally suggests melatonin. The evidence conflicts. ^[apa-delirium-2025, padis-2025]

## Monitor
- Reassess delirium at least every shift and with any change: {{calc:4at}} or {{calc:cam-icu}}, and {{calc:rass}} in the ICU. ^[padis-2018, nice-delirium-2023]
- After any antipsychotic: sedation, blood pressure, extrapyramidal signs, and ECG with {{calc:qtc}} after dose increases or if other QT-prolonging drugs are added. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010, label-haloperidol-inj-2026]
- Expected course: days to weeks. Resolution lags the cause. ^[common-practice]
- Daily review of the antipsychotic, benzodiazepine and anticholinergic orders. Stop as the delirium clears. ^[apa-delirium-2025]

## Escalation
- Psychiatry or geriatrics: severe agitation not controlled with the lowest antipsychotic dose, diagnostic uncertainty between delirium and a primary psychiatric disorder, catatonia, or a patient with dementia. ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]
- Neurology: focal deficits, suspected seizure or nonconvulsive status, encephalitis. ^[common-practice]
- ICU: hemodynamic instability, respiratory failure, or agitation that threatens airway or lines despite measures. ^[common-practice]
- Palliative care or goals-of-care discussion: delirium at the end of life or in prolonged critical illness. ^[common-practice]

## More Information
### Where Guidelines Differ: Antipsychotics, melatonin
:::compare Antipsychotics to treat delirium
- APA 2025: for severe distress or risk of harm after de-escalation fails. Not to prevent or shorten delirium. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
- SCCM PADIS 2025: no recommendation for or against (the 2018 guideline advised against routine use). ^[padis-2025, padis-2018]
:::

:::compare Melatonin
- APA 2025: do not use melatonin or ramelteon to prevent or treat delirium. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
- SCCM PADIS 2025: conditional suggestion to use melatonin in the ICU (low certainty). ^[padis-2025]
:::

### Special Populations: Older adults, postoperative, dementia
- Older adults: Beers lists anticholinergics, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines, Z-drugs, corticosteroids, H2 blockers and opioids as drugs to avoid in those with or at high risk of delirium. Antipsychotics only if nonpharmacologic options failed and there is substantial risk of harm. ^[ags-beers-2023]
- Postoperative: ^[esaic-pod-2024]
  - No drug prophylaxis ^[esaic-pod-2024]
  - Multicomponent nonpharmacologic care for all at-risk patients ^[esaic-pod-2024]
  - Low-dose haloperidol only if nonpharmacologic measures fail ^[esaic-pod-2024]
- Dementia: antipsychotics carry a boxed warning for increased mortality. ^[label-haloperidol-inj-2026]

### Complications: Outcomes
- Falls, pressure injury, aspiration, longer stay, loss of independence, and persistent cognitive decline. ^[apa-delirium-2025, common-practice]

### After the Acute Phase: Discharge
- Document delirium in the discharge summary and tell the family. Arrange follow-up of cognition. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
- Review medicines at transitions and stop antipsychotics started in hospital. ^[apa-delirium-2025]

### Evidence: Key trials
- AID-ICU (2022): IV haloperidol in ICU delirium did not significantly raise days alive and out of hospital at 90 days. 90-day mortality was lower in the haloperidol group (secondary). ^[aid-icu-2022]
- MIND-USA (2018): IV haloperidol and ziprasidone did not increase days alive without delirium or coma versus placebo (89% hypoactive delirium). ^[mind-usa-2018]
- A2B (2025): dexmedetomidine did not speed extubation compared with propofol and caused more agitation and bradycardia. ^[a2b-2025]
- HELP meta-analysis (2018): multicomponent programs lowered delirium incidence (OR 0.47). ^[help-meta-2018]

### Why the Treatment Order: Cause and environment first
- Delirium is a symptom of a physiologic insult. Treating the cause and removing deliriogenic drugs works, and drugs treat only the behavior. ^[apa-delirium-2025, nice-delirium-2023]

## Pearls
- Hypoactive delirium is missed until someone screens for it. ^[common-practice]
- Never start with an antipsychotic. Look for pain, retention, a missing hearing aid or a new drug. ^[nice-delirium-2023, common-practice]
- Do not give lorazepam to calm delirium unless the cause is alcohol or sedative withdrawal. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
- Check the {{calc:qtc}} before and after haloperidol, and correct potassium and magnesium. ^[aha-accf-qt-2010]
- Stop antipsychotics before discharge. ^[apa-delirium-2025]
