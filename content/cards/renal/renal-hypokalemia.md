## At a Glance
- Severity: mild {{threshold:k-hypo-mild}}, moderate {{threshold:k-hypo-moderate}}, severe {{threshold:k-hypo-severe}}. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- ECG in all. IV K⁺ with continuous ECG for K⁺ {{threshold:k-hypo-severe}}, neuromuscular symptoms or ECG changes. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Check and replete Mg: hypomagnesemia makes hypokalemia refractory. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- Oral KCl preferred. IV KCl in saline, not dextrose, never as a push. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, label-kcl-injection-2026]
- Cause by urine K⁺ and acid–base status. Hypokalemia with hypertension: screen for primary aldosteronism. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]

## Severity
:::scale K⁺
- Mild: {{threshold:k-hypo-mild}} ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Moderate: {{threshold:k-hypo-moderate}} ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Severe: {{threshold:k-hypo-severe}} ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
:::

- Symptoms usually only below 3.0 mEq/L, unless the fall is rapid or the patient takes digoxin. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Emergency: K⁺ {{threshold:k-hypo-severe}}, arrhythmia, ECG changes, paralysis or respiratory muscle weakness. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- Higher arrhythmia risk: ischemic heart disease, digoxin. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

## Causes
- **Pseudohypokalemia:** marked leukocytosis with delayed processing (cells take up K⁺ in the tube). Repeat promptly or check a blood gas K⁺. ^[common-practice]
- **Shift into cells:** insulin, β₂-agonists, refeeding, thyrotoxic or familial periodic paralysis. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, aspen-refeeding-2020]
- **GI losses:** diarrhea, laxatives, villous adenoma. Vomiting and NG suction (mostly renal loss from alkalosis). ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, common-practice]
- **Renal losses, normal BP:** [!most-common] loop and thiazide diuretics. Hypomagnesemia. RTA, DKA. Bartter and Gitelman syndromes. Amphotericin B, high-dose penicillins. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- **Renal losses with hypertension:** [!cannot-miss] primary aldosteronism. Diuretics in a hypertensive patient. Renovascular disease, renin-secreting tumor. Cushing syndrome. Licorice, apparent mineralocorticoid excess. Congenital adrenal hyperplasia. Liddle syndrome. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
- **Poor intake:** rarely the sole cause. Usually adds to another. ^[common-practice]

## Presentation
- Weakness, cramps, ascending paralysis. Constipation, ileus. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Severe: rhabdomyolysis, respiratory failure. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- ECG: ST depression, T-wave flattening, prominent U waves (V4–V6), QT (QU) prolongation. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
{{media:ecg-hypokalemia}}
- Arrhythmias: atrial and ventricular ectopy, AV block, VT, VF. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Kidney: nephrogenic diabetes insipidus (polyuria), tubulointerstitial nephritis. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

## Diagnosis
#### First line
- BMP, Mg, phosphorus, calcium, glucose. ECG. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Spot urine K⁺ and creatinine. Urine Cl⁻ when alkalotic. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Blood gas if the cause is unclear. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Digoxin level if on digoxin. TSH with tachycardia or suspected periodic paralysis. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

#### Interpretation
- Urine K⁺:creatinine {{threshold:urine-k-cr-renal-loss}}: renal loss. Lower suggests GI loss, shift or poor intake. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

| Acid–base | ↓ urine K⁺ | ↑ urine K⁺ |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolic acidosis | Lower GI loss (diarrhea, laxatives, villous adenoma) | DKA, RTA (proximal or distal) ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018] |
| Metabolic alkalosis | Vomiting, prior diuretic use | Normal BP: diuretics, vomiting, Bartter or Gitelman. Hypertension: mineralocorticoid excess ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018] |

- Alkalosis with renal K⁺ loss: low urine Cl⁻ suggests vomiting. Normal urine Cl⁻ suggests Bartter or Gitelman (or active diuretic). ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Hypertension: aldosterone, renin and aldosterone-to-renin ratio. ^[endocrine-aldosteronism-2025, aha-acc-bp-2025, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

3. Treat the cause
   - Stop or reduce kaliuretic drugs ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
   - Treat diarrhea or vomiting ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
   - Control glycosuria ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
   - Diuretic still needed: add a K⁺-sparing diuretic. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
   - With a K⁺-sparing diuretic, ACE inhibitor or ARB: stop or reduce KCl and check K⁺ often (KCl labels contraindicate amiloride and triamterene). ^[label-klor-con-2025, label-kcl-oral-solution-2026]
   - Primary aldosteronism: mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist or surgery. ^[endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]

- KCl is the preferred salt. Potassium phosphate with concurrent hypophosphatemia, bicarbonate or citrate with metabolic acidosis. See [[renal-electrolyte-repletion/choosing|Electrolyte Repletion]]. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]

## Monitor
:::times Recheck K⁺ no sooner than ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- 1 h after IV
- 2 h after oral
:::

- K⁺ <3.0 mEq/L on IV replacement: recheck every 2–4 h until >3.5 mEq/L. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- Mild to moderate: K⁺ daily, adjusting the dose. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Serum K⁺ poorly reflects total-body deficit. Dose by repeated levels. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Refractory: recheck Mg. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]

## Escalation
- Nephrology: dialysis or severe kidney impairment needing K⁺ replacement. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Critical care: K⁺ {{threshold:k-hypo-severe}} with arrhythmia, paralysis or respiratory muscle weakness. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- Endocrinology: suspected primary aldosteronism or other mineralocorticoid excess. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]

## More Information
### Special Populations: Kidney disease, heart disease, DKA
- Severe kidney impairment or dialysis: replace very cautiously with nephrology. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- GFR {{threshold:gfr-severe-impairment}}: start low and recheck K⁺ often, every 2–4 h on IV replacement (risk of life-threatening hyperkalemia, the IV KCl concentrate and premixed-bag labels list renal failure as a contraindication). ^[label-kcl-injection-2026, label-kcl-premix-2018, label-klor-con-2025, asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- Heart failure or myocardial infarction: K⁺ ≥4.0 mEq/L is the long-standing advice. See [[renal-electrolyte-repletion/indications|Electrolyte Repletion]] for the evidence. ^[cohn-potassium-2000, goyal-potassium-ami-2012]
- DKA: K⁺ <3.5 mEq/L: replete first and delay insulin until K⁺ is >3.5 mEq/L. ^[ada-hyperglycemic-crises-2024]

### Complications: Arrhythmia, rhabdomyolysis, rebound
- Arrhythmia and sudden death, especially with ischemic heart disease or digoxin. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Rhabdomyolysis, paralysis, respiratory failure. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Treatment: rebound hyperkalemia, especially with IV K⁺ or a transcellular shift. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]

### After the Acute Phase: Cause, prevention
- Even mild chronic hypokalemia is linked to CKD progression, hypertension and higher mortality. Evaluate and treat it. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012]
- On diuretics: K⁺ supplement or a K⁺-sparing diuretic. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, label-klor-con-2025]

{{dose:kcl-oral-prevention}}

- Hypertension: ^[aha-acc-bp-2025, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
  - Screen for primary aldosteronism with aldosterone and renin ^[aha-acc-bp-2025, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
  - In resistant hypertension, screen even with normal K⁺ (only 20–50% of primary aldosteronism has spontaneous hypokalemia). Continue antihypertensives other than MRAs for the initial screen ^[aha-acc-bp-2025, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
  - β-blockers and central α₂-agonists suppress renin (false positive), while ACE inhibitors, ARBs, diuretics and MRAs raise it (false negative) ^[aha-acc-bp-2025, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
  - Repeat off the drug if the result is discordant and stopping is safe ^[aha-acc-bp-2025, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]

### Epidemiology and Pathophysiology: Who gets it, K⁺ handling
- Up to 20% of hospitalized patients. Clinically significant in 4–5%. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Common on loop and thiazide diuretics. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Over half of clinically significant hypokalemia has concomitant Mg deficiency. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]

## Pearls
- Refractory hypokalemia: replete Mg first. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Dextrose-containing carrier fluid can worsen hypokalemia. ^[asmar-hypokalemia-2012, kardalas-hypokalemia-2018]
- Hypokalemia with hypertension: think aldosterone. ^[kardalas-hypokalemia-2018, endocrine-aldosteronism-2025]
- Correcting hypokalemia raises serum Na⁺, and hypokalemia raises the risk of osmotic demyelination: watch the rate in coexisting hyponatremia. ^[rondon-berrios-hypertonic-2022, verbalis-hyponatremia-2013]
