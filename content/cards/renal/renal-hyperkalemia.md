## Key points
- Severity guides treatment: mild {{threshold:k-mild}}, moderate {{threshold:k-moderate}}, severe {{threshold:k-severe}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Get an urgent 12-lead ECG at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- A normal ECG does not rule out danger. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- With ECG changes, give IV calcium first; it protects the heart but does not lower potassium. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Insulin with dextrose is the most effective way to shift potassium into cells; watch for hypoglycemia for 6 hours. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Only binders, diuretics and dialysis remove potassium from the body; recheck potassium for rebound. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Definition and classification
- Commonly defined as potassium of 5.5 mEq/L or higher, though cutoffs vary between laboratories and between serum and plasma. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- *Acute* hyperkalemia: above the upper limit of normal and not known to be chronic. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Epidemiology and risk factors
### Summary
Common with reduced kidney function, diabetes, heart failure and drugs that block the renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

- Main risk factors: lower eGFR, a higher baseline potassium, diabetes, heart failure and coronary disease. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- In ICU patients, prevalence rises with AKI stage: about 3% without AKI to about 32% at stage 3. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Drugs: potassium-sparing diuretics, mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors, beta-blockers, NSAIDs, heparin, digoxin and succinylcholine. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole can also raise potassium. ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- AKI often causes hyperkalemia, especially when oliguric; acidosis, catabolism and tissue breakdown add to the potassium release. ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- Other causes: rhabdomyolysis and major tissue injury, potassium supplements and salt substitutes. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Pathophysiology
### Summary
Results from potassium shifting out of cells, reduced kidney excretion, or (rarely alone) high intake. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

- High intake alone rarely causes hyperkalemia because healthy kidneys can excrete large loads. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- High potassium depolarizes cardiac cells, causing progressive conduction changes and arrhythmias. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Clinical presentation
- Often asymptomatic. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Muscle weakness that typically ascends from the legs to the trunk; paresthesias and fasciculations may come first; paralysis can occur. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ECG changes, roughly in order: peaked T waves, then smaller P waves, longer PR and wider QRS, then a sine-wave pattern. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- The ECG is insensitive: up to half of patients have a normal ECG, even with very high potassium. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ECG findings linked to bad outcomes: QRS prolongation, symptomatic bradycardia and junctional rhythms. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Changes can mimic ST-elevation myocardial infarction or Brugada pattern. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Differential diagnosis
| Diagnosis | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| Pseudohyperkalemia | Hemolyzed sample, prolonged tourniquet or fist clenching, clotting, very high white cell or platelet count, or delayed or cold storage of clotted samples; potassium normal on a careful repeat sample ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026] |

## Diagnostic evaluation
- If the result doesn't fit the patient, consider pseudohyperkalemia and repeat from a large vein with gentle traction and prompt analysis. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- In an emergency, a blood gas potassium gives a fast result while waiting for the laboratory value. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Point-of-care results differ from the laboratory by about 0.1–0.5 mEq/L and can't detect hemolysis; confirm with the laboratory or a second point-of-care test. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Urgent 12-lead ECG at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Look for the cause: medication review, kidney function, hemolysis screen and, if relevant, the cause of AKI. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Risk stratification
- Continuous cardiac monitoring for {{threshold:k-severe}}, any ECG changes, or {{threshold:k-moderate}} in a patient who is unwell or expected to rise quickly. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Severe hyperkalemia needs urgent hospital assessment and a monitored bed. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Management
{{pathway:hyperkalemia-management}}

#### Step 1: Protect the heart
- With ECG changes, give {{dose:calcium-gluconate-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- In cardiac arrest or peri-arrest, calcium chloride is preferred: {{dose:calcium-chloride-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- KDIGO suggests giving calcium at {{threshold:k-calcium-without-ecg}} when no ECG or monitor is available yet. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- With a normal ECG, IV calcium is not recommended. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- In digoxin toxicity, calcium was historically avoided, but recent studies found no harm from giving it. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Calcium works within 1–3 minutes and lasts 30–60 minutes; repeat if changes persist or recur. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- The evidence for IV calcium is weak, and its grade was lowered in 2026 after an international review. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

#### Step 2: Shift potassium into cells
- Severe: {{dose:insulin-regular-hyperkalemia}} with {{dose:dextrose-50-hyperkalemia}} (recommended); moderate: suggested. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- If glucose was {{threshold:glucose-hypoglycemia-risk}} before treatment, follow with {{dose:dextrose-10-infusion-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- With glucose {{threshold:glucose-skip-dextrose}}, insulin may be given without dextrose. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Insulin dose is debated: KDIGO notes a lower dose lowered potassium similarly with less hypoglycemia; the UK guideline found the standard dose more effective and doesn't recommend reducing it. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Add {{dose:albuterol-neb-hyperkalemia}} for severe hyperkalemia; never use albuterol alone for severe hyperkalemia. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Sodium bicarbonate is not routine; consider it only with metabolic acidosis in patients who can take the sodium load. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

#### Step 3: Remove potassium
- Severe: {{dose:szc-hyperkalemia-initial}} is recommended by the UK guideline; moderate: consider it. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- US labels say potassium binders are not emergency treatment for life-threatening hyperkalemia because of their delayed onset. ^[label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]
- The UK guideline suggests patiromer as an option (onset about 7 hours): {{dose:patiromer-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, label-veltassa-2025]
- KDIGO advises against patiromer for acute treatment. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- The UK guideline no longer routinely uses calcium polystyrene sulfonate for acute hyperkalemia. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Never give sodium polystyrene sulfonate with sorbitol. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Loop diuretic if the patient makes urine, especially with volume overload: {{dose:furosemide-hyperkalemia}}. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- On dialysis with severe hyperkalemia: urgent dialysis. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Refractory hyperkalemia or oliguric kidney failure: nephrology to consider dialysis. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

#### Cardiac arrest from hyperkalemia
- IV calcium, sodium bicarbonate, and insulin with glucose may be reasonable (weak recommendations); inhaled albuterol is not recommended during arrest. ^[aha-special-circumstances-2025]

### More detail on treatment timing
| Treatment | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| IV calcium | 1–3 min | 30–60 min ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020] |
| Insulin with dextrose | About 1 h | Variable ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| Nebulized albuterol | About 30 min | About 2 h ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| IV furosemide | About 15 min | 2–3 h ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| Hemodialysis | Immediate | Variable ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |

## Monitoring
- Potassium at least 1, 2, 4, 6 and 24 hours after treatment of moderate or severe hyperkalemia. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Expect rebound after 2–3 hours if potassium hasn't been removed from the body. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Glucose at 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, 300 and 360 minutes after insulin-glucose. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Complications
- Arrhythmias and sudden cardiac death. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Treatment-related: hypoglycemia after insulin, rebound hyperkalemia, edema from the sodium in sodium zirconium cyclosilicate, low magnesium with patiromer. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]

## Specialist involvement
- Renal or critical care team for an urgent opinion for severe hyperkalemia, guided by the clinical picture. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Nephrology for anyone on dialysis, and when hyperkalemia doesn't respond or occurs with AKI. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Critical care for airway, breathing, circulation or consciousness problems, or when escalation is needed. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Transitions of care
- Review drugs that raise potassium during the admission, and weigh the risk of recurrence before restarting them. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- UK guideline: withhold renin–angiotensin–aldosterone system inhibitors during acute illness (such as sepsis, hypovolemia or AKI) at any severity. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Stop them at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}} in patients who can't take a potassium binder. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- KDIGO: consider stopping them temporarily; for chronic hyperkalemia, binders can help keep these drugs going. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Low-potassium dietary advice for moderate or severe kidney disease when hyperkalemia persists, after non-dietary causes are addressed. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Arrange a potassium blood test after discharge for everyone treated for hyperkalemia in hospital. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Maintenance binder if needed: {{dose:szc-hyperkalemia-maintenance}} or {{dose:patiromer-hyperkalemia}}. ^[label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]

## Pearls and pitfalls
- Calcium buys time but lowers no potassium; pair it with treatment that shifts and removes potassium. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- A normal ECG is not reassuring when the potassium is truly high. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Shifting treatments wear off; recheck for rebound. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Check glucose before insulin and for 6 hours after. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
