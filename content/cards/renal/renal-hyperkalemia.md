## Key points
- Severity: mild {{threshold:k-mild}}, moderate {{threshold:k-moderate}}, severe {{threshold:k-severe}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- STAT 12-lead ECG at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- A normal ECG does not exclude life-threatening hyperkalemia. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ECG changes: IV calcium first for cardiac membrane stabilization; does not lower K⁺. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Insulin-dextrose is the most effective intracellular shift; monitor for hypoglycemia for 6 h. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Only binders, diuretics and dialysis eliminate K⁺; recheck K⁺ for rebound. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Definition and classification
- Commonly K⁺ ≥5.5 mEq/L; cutoffs vary by laboratory and by serum vs plasma. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- *Acute* hyperkalemia: K⁺ above the upper limit of normal, not known to be chronic. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Epidemiology and risk factors
### Summary
Common with reduced GFR, diabetes, heart failure and RAAS inhibition. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

- Risk factors: lower eGFR, higher baseline K⁺, diabetes, heart failure, CAD. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ICU prevalence rises with AKI stage: ~3% without AKI, ~32% at stage 3. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Drugs: K⁺-sparing diuretics, MRAs, ACE inhibitors, ARBs, direct renin inhibitors, β-blockers, NSAIDs, heparin, digoxin, succinylcholine. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole. ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- AKI, especially oliguric; acidosis, catabolism and tissue breakdown add to K⁺ release. ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- Rhabdomyolysis and tissue injury; K⁺ supplements and salt substitutes. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Pathophysiology
### Summary
Transcellular shift, impaired renal excretion, or (rarely alone) excess intake. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

- Excess intake rarely causes hyperkalemia with normal renal K⁺ excretion. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Myocyte depolarization causes progressive conduction delay and arrhythmia. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Clinical presentation
- Often asymptomatic. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Ascending weakness (legs to trunk); paresthesias and fasciculations may precede; paralysis. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ECG progression: peaked T waves → P-wave flattening, PR prolongation, QRS widening → sine wave. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- ECG is insensitive: up to half have a normal ECG, even with marked hyperkalemia. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- High-risk ECG findings: QRS prolongation, symptomatic bradycardia, junctional rhythm. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- May mimic STEMI or Brugada pattern. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Differential diagnosis
| Diagnosis | Distinguishing features |
|---|---|
| Pseudohyperkalemia | Hemolysis, prolonged tourniquet or fist clenching, clotted sample, marked leukocytosis or thrombocytosis, delayed or cold storage; normal K⁺ on careful repeat ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026] |

## Diagnostic evaluation
- Result discordant with the clinical picture: repeat from a large vein, gentle traction, prompt analysis. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Emergency: blood gas K⁺ while awaiting the laboratory value. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Point-of-care K⁺ differs from laboratory by ~0.1–0.5 mEq/L and does not detect hemolysis; confirm by laboratory or repeat point-of-care test. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- STAT 12-lead ECG at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Etiology: medication review, renal function, hemolysis screen, cause of AKI if present. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

## Risk stratification
- Continuous cardiac monitoring: {{threshold:k-severe}}, any ECG changes, or {{threshold:k-moderate}} if unwell or rising rapidly. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Severe hyperkalemia: urgent hospital assessment, monitored bed. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Management
{{pathway:hyperkalemia-management}}

#### Step 1: Cardiac membrane stabilization
- ECG changes: {{dose:calcium-gluconate-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Cardiac arrest or peri-arrest: calcium chloride preferred, {{dose:calcium-chloride-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- KDIGO: give calcium at {{threshold:k-calcium-without-ecg}} if no ECG or monitor is yet available. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Normal ECG: IV calcium not recommended. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Digoxin toxicity: historical concern about calcium; recent studies show no harm. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Onset 1–3 min, duration 30–60 min; repeat if ECG changes persist or recur. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Evidence for IV calcium is weak; grade lowered in 2026 after international review. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

#### Step 2: Intracellular shift
- Severe: {{dose:insulin-regular-hyperkalemia}} with {{dose:dextrose-50-hyperkalemia}} (recommended); moderate: suggested. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Pre-treatment glucose {{threshold:glucose-hypoglycemia-risk}}: follow with {{dose:dextrose-10-infusion-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Glucose {{threshold:glucose-skip-dextrose}}: insulin may be given without dextrose. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Insulin dose is debated: KDIGO notes a lower dose with similar K⁺ lowering and less hypoglycemia; UK guideline found the standard dose more effective and does not recommend reduction. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Severe: add {{dose:albuterol-neb-hyperkalemia}}; never albuterol alone. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Sodium bicarbonate: not routine; consider only with metabolic acidosis if the sodium load is tolerated. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]

#### Step 3: Elimination
- Severe: {{dose:szc-hyperkalemia-initial}} (UK guideline recommends); moderate: consider. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- US labels: K⁺ binders are not emergency treatment for life-threatening hyperkalemia (delayed onset). ^[label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]
- Patiromer: UK guideline option (onset ~7 h), {{dose:patiromer-hyperkalemia}}. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, label-veltassa-2025]
- KDIGO advises against patiromer for acute treatment. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Calcium polystyrene sulfonate: no longer routine for acute hyperkalemia (UK guideline). ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Never sodium polystyrene sulfonate with sorbitol. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Non-oliguric, especially with volume overload: {{dose:furosemide-hyperkalemia}}. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025]
- On maintenance dialysis with severe hyperkalemia: urgent dialysis. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Refractory hyperkalemia or oliguric AKI: nephrology to consider dialysis. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

#### Hyperkalemic cardiac arrest
- IV calcium, sodium bicarbonate, and insulin-dextrose may be reasonable (weak recommendations); inhaled albuterol not recommended during arrest. ^[aha-special-circumstances-2025]

### More detail on treatment timing
| Treatment | Onset | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| IV calcium | 1–3 min | 30–60 min ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020] |
| Insulin-dextrose | ~1 h | Variable ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| Nebulized albuterol | ~30 min | ~2 h ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| IV furosemide | ~15 min | 2–3 h ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |
| Hemodialysis | Immediate | Variable ^[emcreg-hyperkalemia-2025] |

## Monitoring
- K⁺ at least at 1, 2, 4, 6 and 24 h after treatment of moderate or severe hyperkalemia. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Rebound at 2–3 h if K⁺ has not been eliminated. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Glucose at 0, 30, 60, 90, 120, 180, 240, 300 and 360 min after insulin-dextrose. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Complications
- Arrhythmia, sudden cardiac death. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Treatment-related: hypoglycemia (insulin), rebound hyperkalemia, edema (sodium load of SZC), hypomagnesemia (patiromer). ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]

## Specialist involvement
- Severe hyperkalemia: urgent renal or critical care opinion, guided by the clinical picture. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Nephrology: maintenance dialysis, refractory hyperkalemia, hyperkalemia with AKI. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026, kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Critical care: airway, breathing, circulation or consciousness compromise, or need for escalation. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]

## Transitions of care
- Review K⁺-raising drugs during admission; weigh recurrence risk before restarting. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- UK guideline: withhold RAAS inhibitors during acute illness (sepsis, hypovolemia, AKI) at any severity. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Stop RAAS inhibitors at {{threshold:k-ecg-monitoring}} if a K⁺ binder cannot be used. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- KDIGO: consider temporary discontinuation; in chronic hyperkalemia, binders can enable continued RAAS inhibition. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Low-K⁺ diet for persistent hyperkalemia with moderate–severe CKD, after non-dietary causes are addressed. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020, ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Repeat K⁺ after discharge for all patients treated in hospital. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
- Maintenance binder if needed: {{dose:szc-hyperkalemia-maintenance}} or {{dose:patiromer-hyperkalemia}}. ^[label-lokelma-2024, label-veltassa-2025]

## Pearls and pitfalls
- Calcium buys time but lowers no K⁺; pair with shift and elimination. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- A normal ECG is not reassuring with true hyperkalemia. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Shift therapies wear off; recheck for rebound. ^[kdigo-acute-hyperkalemia-2020]
- Check glucose before insulin and for 6 h after. ^[ukka-hyperkalaemia-2026]
