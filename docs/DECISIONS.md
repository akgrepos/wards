# Decisions

Settled choices for Wards, newest first. Each entry says what was decided and why.

| Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-18 | **Drug interactions: link out, no built-in checker.** The app links to Medscape's free Drug Interaction Checker (no sign-in; clinician-oriented, with mechanism and action grades) from Library › Tools and from every dose sheet; it opens in Safari and needs internet, like the MDCalc links. Each medication still lists its major interactions from the official label (DATA-TYPES "interaction"). | NIH's free interaction API (RxNav) was discontinued on 2024-01-02 and no free, maintained replacement exists; a live API would break offline use and send lookups to an outside server; a homemade checker that misses interactions is unsafe. |
| 2026-10-18 | **App redesign approved** (after six rounds of mock-ups and an independent review; private mock-up canvas). Four tabs in a floating glass bar: **Home** (Continue Reading, four editable emergency shortcuts, Favorites, Recent), **Emergencies** (act now: Do Now steps with one drug per dose row, Timed Mode), **Topics** (systems; full topic pages), **Library** (scores and calculators, criteria and staging, medications, pathways, labs and tests), plus a separate round search button. Emergency and topic pages link to each other with one icon-only button at top right (book or bolt) that opens a pop-up first, then the full page. **Timed Mode** is full screen; its timer stays in a bar above the tab bar on every screen until ended; ending needs confirmation and shows a summary of what was given and when (kept on the phone only). Topic pages: Contents menu plus collapsible section headers (no pill row); sections At a Glance, Severity, Causes, Presentation, Diagnosis, Treatment, Monitor, When to Call, **More Information** (guidelines differ, special populations, complications, after the acute phase, evidence, why the treatment order, epidemiology and pathophysiology), Pearls, Related, Sources. One source line per section; dose sheets for every dose; one pop-up at a time; blue for main actions, red only for the emergency color and destructive actions. Clinical text in the mock-ups is sample only. | Cards read as walls of text and the app felt barebones; the app must serve both a code at the bedside and deep clinical reasoning. Supersedes the 2026-10-17 card display and navigation choices where they differ; CARD-TYPES.md and the design system get updated as part of the build. |
| 2026-10-18 | **Repo re-created:** the GitHub repo was rebuilt under the same name and address with the cleaned history (the one commit saved under "AG" now says `akgrepos`), every setting was restored (rebase-only merges, protected `main`, publishing from `main` only, GitHub-only actions, security alerts and fixes, secret scanning), and the old repo was deleted. Pull request numbers restart at 1; the old pull request pages are gone, but every change they made is in the files. | GitHub kept hidden copies of the original commits behind the first pull requests, showing the owner's name and computer email. Deleting the old repo was the only sure way to remove them. Settles the first finding of the 2026-10-17 systems audit. |
| 2026-10-17 | **End-of-chat wrap-up:** when the owner says to wrap up, Claude follows the `wards-wrap-up` skill (kept in the owner's private Claude settings, triggered by plain words, no slash command): finish or park work, stop servers and close test tabs, reset the simulator, clean Git and temp files, review artifacts (deletions confirmed with the owner), update STATUS/DECISIONS/CLAUDE.md/memory, write a "Start here next time" handoff, and mark the chat [Done]. | Each new chat starts from a clean, documented state. |
| 2026-10-17 | **All visual and interaction design is checked against the design system and Apple's HIG** (UI, UX, icons, markers, layout, motion). Anything the design system lacks is added to it first, kept generic, then copied into Wards. | Consistent, native-feeling app; one shared design system. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Source markers in the app:** a line resting on common clinical practice shows a stethoscope in place of a source pill (tap: "Common clinical practice."); a last-resort line checked only with a clinical reference shows a green check (tap: "Verified with a clinical reference."). Both are excluded from the card's Sources list. Built as a `marker` option on the design system's SourcePill. | The owner can tell at a glance what is cited, what is standard practice, and what was only checked, without naming non-citable references. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Criteria sets** (e.g. KDIGO AKI staging) are a data type: shown in full on their own line, or as a tappable name inside a sentence. | One home for formal definitions, reused across cards. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Sources, updated (supersedes the earlier "Sources" entry where they differ).** (1) Cite the primary guideline or study: current US society guidelines first, newest *final* edition (public-review drafts are not cited; note their changes for later); European guidelines noted where they differ. (2) The reference handbook guides topic choice, organization, **wording, terminology and what to include**, in its terse clinical register, but is never named, cited or copied. (3) AMBOSS is a search tool: its reference list (the numbered references beside each statement) is the first place to find primary sources. If that primary source is paywalled, trust AMBOSS and cite the primary source without verifying it further. (4) **Common clinical practice** (including standard practice from the handbook) may be included without a citation; it shows a stethoscope marker ("Common clinical practice"). (5) **Last resort only:** if no primary source can be found, a line may rest on AMBOSS; it shows a green check ("Verified with a clinical reference"), the AMBOSS article is recorded as a hidden source in the repo (name and link only; nothing is downloaded or copied), and the owner is told each time. (6) OpenEvidence is for finding and checking (Sackett or Snow mode), never cited. (7) Summarize, never copy, any source. (8) AMBOSS, OpenEvidence and the reference handbook are never named or cited in the app. | Keeps every line traceable while covering standard practice the literature doesn't spell out, and respects AMBOSS's terms. |
| 2026-10-17 | **HIG review (fourth design system pass).** Six reviewers compared the design system and the app with the full Apple HIG (a private text copy and rule summaries are kept outside the repo, with the reference handbook). Chosen and built: sheets that cover the screen wherever you've scrolled, resize by drag and close by swipe; 44pt touch areas on every small control; Escape, tap-outside and Apple's button order for alerts, action sheets, popovers and menus; the title shown once (large in the page, small in the bar after scrolling); Back keeps your scroll position; the app reopens on the last card; the section bar follows and opens collapsed sections; clearer tappable doses and a darker link blue for small text; tables with a fixed first column; the update banner at the bottom with "Later"; dark launch screens; a readable width on wide screens. New design system components: TappableValue, Table, Figure, Banner, BottomBar (search), Highlight, ShareButton, NumberField; TextField labels, units and errors. | Fixes a live bug (sheets opening off-screen) and brings the app closer to how Apple's own apps behave. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Accessibility scope unchanged:** VoiceOver, Reduce Motion and Increase Contrast stay out of scope (2026-10-16 decision). The 44pt touch areas are kept because they're about fingers, not assistive technology. | The owner doesn't need accessibility features; easier tapping helps everyone. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Independent reviewer on every card** (settles the accuracy-check pilot test): after the reverse self-check, a separate reviewer checks each card and its data against the source texts before the pull request. Supersedes "no separate reviewer agent" in the accuracy check below. Pearls and section summaries now need sources too, and the content check enforces it. | On Hyperkalemia the reviewer found 14 problems the self-check missed, one serious (a lower insulin dose the newest UK guideline advises against). |
| 2026-10-17 | **System lists:** each system lists Approach cards first, then topic subsections (e.g. Arrhythmias, Electrolyte Disorders), then Reference; subsections and the cards within each group are alphabetical. Condition cards name their subsection in the file header; the content check enforces it. Supersedes "most common first". | Large systems stay scannable, and alphabetical order is predictable. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Pilot cards:** Hyperkalemia (condition), Acute kidney injury (approach), Electrolyte repletion (reference, protocol), delivered one at a time with the template adjusted after each. | Together they exercise doses, cutoffs, flowcharts, criteria, links and the one-home rule. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Content format and app structure** (details in `docs/CONTENT-FORMAT.md`): cards are Markdown files with a short header and the card type's section headings; shared data items are YAML files; inserts `{{type:id}}`, links `[[card-id]]`, sources `^[id]` at the end of each line. Content is checked on every pull request and bundled into the app at build time, so it works offline. Cards have links of the form `#/card/<id>`, which work offline and when opened directly. Navigation follows Apple's HIG: a home screen with a large title listing the systems and the Library; a system screen grouping cards under Approach, Conditions and Reference; card pages with a back button, a glass section bar and a Sources list. Development-only sample cards live in `content/examples/` and are never published. | Keeps content reviewable as files, enforces the one-home and sourcing rules automatically, and makes every card linkable. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Approved build plans.** Plan A (first): add generic components to the AKG Design System (Flowchart step view and focused map, Source pill with sheet, Content section with "More" expander and section jump bar, Callout) and make all iOS component text follow Text Size; check all four themes; copy into Wards. Plan B (after A): content and data files with automatic checks (broken inserts, unsourced items, non-US units, doses typed outside inserts), card pages with their own links (including direct visits), card display per the specs, flowcharts in cards, and a home list of systems and cards. | Pilot cards need these to be read on the phone; components go in the shared design system per the project rules. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Citation format:** each sourced line ends with a small grey **source pill** showing the short source and year (e.g. "ACC/AHA '23"), with "+1" when a line has more than one source. Tapping a pill opens a half-height sheet (Apple's HIG: sheets, not popovers, on iPhone) with the full citation and an Open source link. Each card ends with a numbered Sources list showing the short form ("Society Year: title"), with the full citation on tap. Sources from inserted items (doses, scores, criteria) merge in automatically, with no duplicates. | Shows where each line comes from and how current it is at a glance, while the full detail stays one tap away. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Interactive flowcharts** (chosen from mock-ups on the simulated iPhone, following Apple's HIG). **Step view:** answered steps stay on screen, stacked and collapsed to "question / your answer" (tap to change); the current step shows its answers as list rows with the interpretation in bold and the criteria in grey underneath (e.g. "Hypotonic" / "Serum osmolality below 275 mOsm/kg"); answers are tapped, never typed. Stage progress bar ("Stage 3 of 5 · Classify"), Start Over, Previous Step. Step kinds: start (one or more entry points), question, range, all-of checklist, caution, result (with lists, side notes and links). Paths may rejoin. A summary of your path at the result. Answers are cleared on leaving the card; nothing is saved. **Map** (toggle beside Step): a focused mini flowchart, top-down with splitting arrows; only your branch is open, other branches are tappable stubs; opens centered on your step. | Handles real-world algorithms (ranges, checklists, rejoins, multiple entries) on a phone, keeps reasoning visible, and keeps the flowchart picture without heavy panning. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Data types** (full spec in `docs/DATA-TYPES.md`): 16 types with one home each, inserted into cards: source, dose, regimen, reference value, threshold, criteria set, score, grading scale, pathway, coverage, media, term, clinical finding, test accuracy, interaction (major only), recommendation (required for any flowchart statement). Structured cards + data: every card section has a checked shape; facts, numbers and reused items are data; explanations stay prose. Inserts look like normal text, and tapping opens their home. Doses show as one line with tap for more; kidney adjustments use the source's measure; optional weight-based dose calculator (nothing saved). Coverage chart: antimicrobials × pathogens with ●/◐/○. Automatic checks reject broken inserts, unsourced items, non-US units and doses typed outside inserts. | One home for every fact keeps cards consistent and errors fixable in one place. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Card types** (full spec in `docs/CARD-TYPES.md`): 8 types (Condition, Approach, Reference with interpretation/protocol/organism/chart/communication variants, Procedure, Medication with a drug-class variant, Score, Physical exam, Diagnostic test), each with fixed sections in a fixed order. Shared header, 3–6 key points, section bar, "More" expanders and footer (pearls, related cards, numbered sources). Recommendation grades not shown. Epidemiology and pathophysiology collapsed by default. Approach cards include common initial orders; procedure cards include a procedure-note checklist. Charts are generated from shared data, never typed. | Every card feels familiar and information is easy to find; the one-home rule holds for doses, coverage and regimens. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Topic organization:** two levels: 16 **Systems** (cardiology, pulmonary, critical care, GI, liver and biliary, kidney/electrolytes/urology, infectious disease, hematology, oncology, endocrinology, rheumatology/MSK, neurology, psychiatry/substance use, skin/allergy/eye/ENT, women's health and pregnancy, hospital medicine) and a **Library** (physical exam, scores and calculators, pathways index, ECG library, imaging library, labs and fluid analysis, procedures, medications). Inside every system: approach cards, then conditions (most common first), then reference cards. One card per thing you'd look up; related cards link instead of repeating. Each card has a permanent ID (`system-topic`). Content is built in waves; Wave 1 is the 30 most common ward problems, and the pilot cards come from it. The working topic list is kept privately until content is added. | Predictable places to look, no duplicated information, and a realistic first batch. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Flowcharts live inside their cards** (their one home). The Library's Pathways page is only an index that opens each card at its flowchart. | Algorithms are where you need them, without copies. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Terminology:** precise medical terminology throughout (e.g. hypoxemia, not "low oxygen"); common synonyms and abbreviations are search aliases only. | Matches clinical language and documentation. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Accuracy check, pilot test:** run both the reverse self-check and an independent reviewer on the pilot cards, count what the reviewer catches that the self-check missed, then choose: self-check only, reviewer for high-risk content (doses, emergencies, pathways), or reviewer on every card. | Decide the cost/benefit from evidence. Refines the accuracy check above. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Conventions:** US units only (reference ranges labeled "typical"); drugs by generic name with common brand names; no documentation/note-writing cards for now. Physical exam references are included: normal vs abnormal findings with documentation-ready wording, and keys for grading scales (e.g. murmur grade, edema), each scale cited to its original source. | Matches US practice; supports writing exam documentation without note templates. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Images:** ECGs are real recordings from open research datasets (e.g. PTB-XL on PhysioNet), drawn on an exactly scaled grid (1 mm/5 mm boxes, 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV) and credited, with criteria cited to their guideline. Diagrams are drawn in the app's style, each citing what it's based on. X-ray, CT and ultrasound images are openly licensed real images, credited; sources that forbid reuse are avoided. | Accurate enough to learn from, fully sourced, legal to publish. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Planning order:** topic list → card types → data types → pilot cards. | Card types define what each card needs; shared data types follow from them. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Content authorship:** Claude writes all clinical content; it must be evidence-based. Entries are treated as verified unless the owner says otherwise; no verification label is shown, only where each piece of information comes from (citation format decided later). | The owner wants a complete, sourced reference without reviewing every entry. Sources on every claim keep it checkable. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Sources:** cite the primary guideline (or study). Current US society guidelines first, the newest edition, with European guidelines noted where they differ meaningfully. The reference handbook (a private copy the owner provides) guides topic choice, organization and what to include, but is never cited. AMBOSS is a search tool; it may be cited directly only if the primary guideline it relies on can't be found; if that guideline is paywalled, cite the guideline anyway, without a label. OpenEvidence is for finding and checking, never cited. PubMed/PMC are used to reach primary sources. Text from any of these is summarized, never copied. | Evidence traces to primary sources; respects copyright. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Internal accuracy check** for every card (not shown in the app): confirm newest guideline; tie every claim to its source while writing; check numbers and doses against the source text; compare with the reference handbook and investigate differences; spot-check key claims with OpenEvidence; double-check doses (guideline or label plus one more source: units, frequency, renal/hepatic adjustment, maximum); a final reverse check by Claude (going source by source to confirm the card matches each source, then listing every number and dose beside its source line), with no separate reviewer agent unless the owner asks for one on a specific card; conflicts are stated on the card or raised with the owner; content PRs carry a short checklist; cards are re-checked when a cited guideline is updated. | Clinical accuracy without the owner reviewing everything. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Scope and depth:** all of internal medicine (inpatient, outpatient, pregnancy-related medical issues). Fuller cards with expandable detail, built for both quick bedside lookup and clinical reasoning (differentials, workups, interpreting labs, imaging and ECGs, assessments and plans, notes). Nothing institution-specific. | One reference for the whole job. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Structure:** a fixed set of card types (e.g. condition, medication, reference, skill), each with a fixed structure and substructure, so every card feels familiar. Every piece of information has one home: shared data (e.g. a dose) lives in one place in a distinct data type and is inserted elsewhere, never rewritten. Cards link to each other. Card types, data types and their structure are still to be designed together. | Easy to find things, fewer errors, no conflicting copies. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Interactive tools:** calculators and scores are interactive (links to MDCalc are fine too). Diagnostic and treatment algorithms are interactive flowcharts in the spirit of Epic Pathways, redrawn in the app's style with sources cited; their design is still to be decided. | Faster and more useful on shift than static text. |
| 2026-10-17 | **Content workflow:** (1) Claude drafts a topic list from the reference handbook and it's settled together; (2) design card types, data types and the pathway format; (3) 2–3 pilot cards read on the phone; (4) adjust; (5) scale in batches. The reference handbook PDF lives in `~/Documents/Wards-reference/`, outside the repo; AMBOSS and OpenEvidence are used through the built-in browser with the owner's own sign-in. | Settles structure before there's content to rework; keeps copyrighted material out of the public repo. |
